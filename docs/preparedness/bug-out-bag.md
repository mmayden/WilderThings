# Bug-Out Bag (72-Hour Kit)

> Assemble a portable kit that sustains you for 72 hours when you must leave home immediately.

## At a Glance

- Keep total pack weight at 15-20% of your body weight — heavier packs cause fatigue and injury.
- Organize gear into tiers: Tier 1 (on your person), Tier 2 (in the bag).
- Test every item before it goes in the bag. Untested gear fails when you need it most.
- Rotate consumables (food, water, batteries, medications) every 6 months.
- A $200 bag built with tested basics beats a $2,000 bag full of untested gadgets.

---

## Purpose and Philosophy

A bug-out bag (BOB) exists for one scenario: you must leave your home quickly and survive for 72 hours without external support. This covers the gap between disaster onset and reaching safety or receiving aid.

The bag is not a wilderness survival kit. It is a grab-and-go system that keeps you hydrated, fed, sheltered, and mobile for three days.

!!! warning "CAUTION"
    A bug-out bag is a supplement to a plan, not a replacement for one. Know your evacuation routes, rally points, and destination before you need them.

## Bag Selection

### Size and Frame

| Body Size | Pack Volume | Frame Type |
|-----------|------------|------------|
| Small adult (under 140 lbs / 64 kg) | 30-40 liters | Internal frame |
| Average adult (140-200 lbs / 64-91 kg) | 40-55 liters | Internal frame |
| Large adult (over 200 lbs / 91 kg) | 50-65 liters | Internal or external frame |

Choose a pack with padded hip belt, sternum strap, and compression straps. The hip belt transfers 70-80% of the load to your hips.

!!! tip
    Try the pack loaded to your target weight before purchasing. Walk at least 1 mile (1.6 km) in it.

## Essential Categories

### Water

- 1 liter (32 oz) minimum carried, plus purification for 3 days.
- Carry a water bottle and a collapsible reservoir.
- Include purification tablets or a squeeze filter.
- See [Water Purification](../water/purification.md) for methods.

### Shelter

- Emergency bivy or lightweight tarp — 8 x 10 feet (2.4 x 3 m) minimum.
- Paracord, 50 feet (15 m).
- Emergency space blankets (2 minimum).

### Fire

- Primary: butane lighter.
- Secondary: ferrocerium rod with striker.
- Tinder: cotton balls with petroleum jelly in a waterproof container.

### Food

- 2,000-3,000 calories per day for 3 days.
- Calorie-dense, shelf-stable options: energy bars, freeze-dried meals, peanut butter packets, trail mix.
- Include a metal cup or pot for boiling water.

### Medical

- Compact first aid kit: adhesive bandages, gauze, medical tape, tourniquet, nitrile gloves, pain relievers, personal medications.
- See [First Aid Basics](../medical/first-aid-basics.md) for kit details.

### Tools

- Fixed-blade knife, 4-5 inch (10-13 cm) blade.
- Multi-tool with pliers.
- Headlamp with spare batteries.
- Duct tape wrapped around a pencil (saves space).

### Documents

- Copies of ID, insurance, and medical records in a waterproof bag.
- Emergency contact list (written — phones die).
- Cash in small bills ($200-500 minimum).

### Communications

- Battery bank and charging cables.
- AM/FM weather radio (hand-crank preferred).
- Whistle on the chest strap.

## Weight Budget

Calculate your maximum pack weight:

1. Weigh yourself in pounds or kilograms.
2. Multiply by 0.15 (minimum) to 0.20 (maximum).
3. That number is your loaded pack weight limit.

**Example:** A 180 lb (82 kg) person carries 27-36 lbs (12-16 kg) maximum.

| Category | Target Weight % |
|----------|----------------|
| Water | 20-25% |
| Shelter | 15-20% |
| Food | 15-20% |
| Medical | 5-10% |
| Tools | 10-15% |
| Clothing | 10-15% |
| Other (documents, comms) | 5-10% |

## Tier System

### Tier 1: On Your Person

Carry these items in your pockets or on your belt at all times during an evacuation:

- Knife or multi-tool
- Lighter
- Flashlight (small)
- Cash
- ID
- Whistle
- Phone

If separated from your bag, Tier 1 items keep you functional.

### Tier 2: In the Bag

Everything else. Pack with heaviest items closest to your back and centered between shoulder blades and hips.

## Seasonal Considerations

=== "Summer"
    - Extra water capacity (add 1 liter / 32 oz).
    - Sun protection: hat, sunscreen, sunglasses.
    - Insect repellent.
    - Lighter shelter setup — tarp over bivy.

=== "Winter"
    - Insulated layers: base layer, fleece, waterproof shell.
    - Extra calories (increase by 500-1,000 per day).
    - Hand and toe warmers.
    - Insulated sleeping pad (R-value 4+).
    - Heavier shelter: 4-season bivy or tent.

=== "Wet Season"
    - Pack liner (trash compactor bag inside the pack).
    - Extra dry socks (2 pairs minimum).
    - Waterproof matches as fire backup.
    - Quick-dry clothing.

## Maintenance Schedule

| Task | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| Check food expiration dates | Every 6 months |
| Replace water | Every 6 months |
| Test batteries and electronics | Every 6 months |
| Rotate medications | Per expiration date |
| Inspect pack seams and zippers | Annually |
| Do a full shakedown hike | Annually |
| Update documents and cash | Annually |

## Budget Tiers

=== "Budget ($150-300)"
    - Military surplus pack.
    - Emergency bivy and space blankets.
    - Sawyer Mini filter.
    - Ferrocerium rod and lighter.
    - Energy bars and instant oatmeal.
    - Basic first aid kit.

=== "Mid-Range ($300-700)"
    - Quality hiking pack (Osprey, Deuter, Gregory).
    - Lightweight tarp and bivy.
    - Squeeze filter and purification tablets.
    - Freeze-dried meals.
    - Comprehensive first aid kit with tourniquet.
    - Headlamp and battery bank.

=== "Premium ($700+)"
    - Ultralight pack with full suspension.
    - Ultralight tent or hammock system.
    - Multi-fuel stove.
    - Satellite communicator.
    - Solar panel charger.
    - Full trauma kit.

## Common Mistakes

- **Packing too heavy.** If you cannot jog 100 yards (91 m) with your bag, it is too heavy. Strip it down.
- **Never testing gear.** Set up your shelter, start a fire with your kit, and filter water before an emergency forces you to learn.
- **Forgetting medications.** Prescription medications are irreplaceable in a crisis. Rotate a 7-day supply into the bag.
- **No physical fitness.** The best gear cannot compensate for the inability to carry it. Train with your bag regularly.
- **All gear, no plan.** A bag without evacuation routes, rally points, and a communication plan is a heavy backpack, not a survival system.
- **Ignoring climate.** A summer bag in January is dangerous. Adjust seasonally.
