# Everyday Carry (EDC) for Preparedness

> Carry essential survival tools on your person daily so you are never without core capabilities when disaster strikes.

## At a Glance

- The best survival kit is the one you have on you. EDC ensures you always have fire, cutting, light, and signaling.
- Keep EDC items compact enough that you carry them without thinking about it.
- Legal restrictions vary by jurisdiction — know what you can carry where you live and travel.
- Your phone is the single most powerful survival tool you own. Keep it charged.
- Review and adjust your EDC seasonally.

---

## Philosophy

Disasters do not wait for you to grab your bug-out bag. An earthquake, active shooter event, vehicle accident, or sudden weather change can happen while you are at work, in a store, or on your commute. EDC bridges the gap between normalcy and accessing your larger kits.

The core question: if you had to walk home right now with only what is in your pockets and on your belt, could you?

## Essential EDC Items

### The Five Cs (Minimum)

| Capability | Item | Why |
|-----------|------|-----|
| Cutting | Folding knife or multi-tool | Process materials, cut cordage, self-defense |
| Combustion | Mini lighter or ferro rod | Start fire for warmth, signaling, water purification |
| Cover | Emergency space blanket | Retain body heat, improvised shelter, signaling |
| Cordage | 10-20 feet (3-6 m) paracord | Lashing, repairs, shelter construction |
| Container | Metal water bottle | Carry and boil water |

### Key Accessories

- **Whistle.** Three blasts is the universal distress signal. Audible at 1 mile (1.6 km). Weighs nothing.
- **Mini flashlight.** 100+ lumens, pocket-sized. Signal, navigate, assess injuries at night.
- **Button compass.** Backup navigation when your phone dies.
- **Cash.** $50-100 in small bills ($1, $5, $10, $20). ATMs and card readers fail during power outages.
- **Pen and paper.** Leave notes, record information, mark maps.

!!! tip
    Attach a whistle and mini light to your keychain. You carry your keys every day — make them work harder.

## Pocket vs Bag EDC

### Pocket EDC (Always On You)

Fits in pants pockets, jacket pockets, or clipped to belt:

- Folding knife
- Mini flashlight
- Lighter
- Cash
- Phone
- Keys with whistle and mini light

### Bag EDC (Daily Carry Bag or Purse)

Expands capability without adding pocket bulk:

- Multi-tool with pliers
- First aid micro-kit (adhesive bandages, alcohol wipes, pain relievers, personal meds)
- Paracord bracelet or hank
- Space blanket
- Water purification tablets (4-6 tablets)
- Energy bar or gel
- Battery bank with cable
- Bandana (tourniquet, pre-filter, sun protection, sling)

!!! note
    A bandana is one of the most versatile EDC items. It serves as a dust mask, water pre-filter, tourniquet, pot holder, signaling flag, and sun shade.

## Urban vs Rural EDC

=== "Urban EDC"
    - Emphasize communication: charged phone, battery bank.
    - Self-defense awareness (check local laws).
    - Transit card or fare with cash backup.
    - Dust mask (N95) — fires, building collapse, air quality events.
    - Compact rain jacket.
    - Building evacuation: know exits in every building you enter.

=== "Rural EDC"
    - Emphasize navigation: compass, map of the area.
    - Fire starting: full-size lighter plus ferro rod.
    - Fixed-blade knife (legal in most rural areas).
    - 50 feet (15 m) paracord.
    - Water container and purification.
    - Signal mirror.
    - Extra food calories.

## Legal Considerations

!!! warning "CAUTION"
    Knife and tool carry laws vary dramatically by country, state, and city. Ignorance of the law is not a defense. Research your specific jurisdiction before carrying.

General guidelines for the United States:

| Item | Typically Legal | Check Local Law |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| Folding knife under 3 inches (7.5 cm) | Most states | City ordinances vary |
| Fixed-blade knife | Most rural areas | Restricted in many cities |
| Multi-tool | Almost everywhere | Some government buildings prohibit |
| Lighter | Everywhere | None |
| Flashlight | Everywhere | None |

When traveling by air, move all cutting tools and lighters to checked luggage or leave them behind.

## Phone as Survival Tool

Your phone provides more survival capability than any other single item:

- **Communication.** Call, text, or signal for help.
- **Navigation.** GPS works without cell service. Download offline maps (Google Maps, Gaia GPS, OsmAnd) before you need them.
- **Light.** Built-in flashlight.
- **Fire.** Battery can ignite steel wool in an extreme emergency.
- **Information.** Stored first aid guides, survival references, contact numbers.
- **Camera.** Document injuries, record surroundings, photograph maps.

### Keep It Charged

- Carry a battery bank (10,000 mAh minimum) daily.
- Enable battery saver mode when grid power is uncertain.
- Airplane mode extends battery life dramatically when you do not need cellular.
- A dead phone is a brick. Treat battery percentage as a survival resource.

## Seasonal Adjustments

=== "Summer"
    - Sunscreen packet.
    - Extra water or electrolyte tabs.
    - Wide-brim hat or buff.
    - Insect repellent wipe.

=== "Winter"
    - Hand warmers (2 minimum).
    - Lip balm and skin protectant.
    - Extra lighter (cold reduces butane pressure).
    - Emergency bivy in bag EDC.
    - Wool or fleece hat that fits in a pocket.

=== "Wet Season"
    - Waterproof phone pouch.
    - Compact rain poncho in bag.
    - Waterproof match case as fire backup.
    - Extra pair of dry socks in bag.

## Building Your EDC

Start with three items and build from there:

1. **Week 1:** Knife, flashlight, lighter.
2. **Week 2:** Add whistle, cash, battery bank.
3. **Week 3:** Add first aid micro-kit, paracord, space blanket.
4. **Week 4:** Add water purification, compass, bandana.

Carry everything for a full month. Remove what you never use. Replace what fails.

## Common Mistakes

- **Carrying too much.** If your EDC is uncomfortable, you will stop carrying it. Start minimal.
- **Buying expensive gear without testing.** A $15 knife you carry daily beats a $150 knife sitting in a drawer.
- **Forgetting to charge the battery bank.** Plug it in every night. A dead battery bank is dead weight.
- **No first aid items.** Adhesive bandages and pain relievers solve more daily problems than any other EDC item.
- **Ignoring legal restrictions.** A knife confiscated at a courthouse is a knife you do not have.
- **Same EDC year-round.** Winter and summer have different threats. Adjust accordingly.
- **No practice.** Start your fire kit in the backyard. Use your multi-tool for real tasks. Build competence before crisis.
