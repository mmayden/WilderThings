# Vehicle Emergency Kit

> Equip your vehicle with the tools and supplies to survive a breakdown, accident, or stranding in any season.

## At a Glance

- A vehicle kit is your mobile preparedness base — it stays in the vehicle at all times.
- Build around two priorities: getting the vehicle moving again and surviving if it cannot move.
- Seasonal additions are critical. A summer kit in a winter blizzard is useless.
- Store water and food carefully — extreme vehicle temperatures degrade both.
- Check and rotate your kit every 6 months at minimum.

---

## Baseline Kit (All Vehicles, All Seasons)

Every vehicle should carry these items year-round, regardless of climate or vehicle type.

### Mechanical Emergency

- Jumper cables or a lithium jump starter.
- Tire repair kit (plug kit + CO2 inflator) or full-size spare with jack and lug wrench.
- Basic tool set: pliers, screwdrivers (flat and Phillips), adjustable wrench, zip ties, electrical tape.
- Tow strap, 20 feet (6 m), rated to at least 1.5x your vehicle weight.
- 1 quart (1 liter) motor oil.
- Duct tape.

### Safety and Signaling

- Reflective warning triangles (3 minimum) or LED road flares.
- High-visibility vest.
- Headlamp with spare batteries (keep hands free while working).
- Fire extinguisher (ABC-rated, minimum 2 lbs / 0.9 kg).
- Seatbelt cutter and window breaker (mount within driver's reach).

### Communication

- Phone charger (12V car adapter and cable).
- Battery bank (10,000+ mAh).
- Written list of emergency contacts (phones die).
- AM/FM radio (most vehicles have one — keep it functional).

### First Aid

- Vehicle first aid kit: adhesive bandages, gauze rolls, medical tape, triangular bandage, nitrile gloves, pain relievers, antihistamines, personal medications.
- Tourniquet (CAT or SOFTT-W).
- Trauma shears.
- See [First Aid Basics](../medical/first-aid-basics.md) for comprehensive kit guidance.

### Shelter and Warmth

- Emergency space blankets (2 per occupant).
- Wool blanket or fleece throw.
- Rain poncho.

### Water and Food

- 1 gallon (3.8 L) of water per occupant.
- Calorie-dense food: energy bars, nuts, jerky (choose items rated for temperature extremes).

!!! warning "CAUTION"
    Water in plastic bottles degrades in extreme heat. Replace vehicle water every 3-6 months. Store in BPA-free containers and keep out of direct sunlight when possible.

### Documents and Cash

- Vehicle registration and insurance copies in a waterproof bag.
- $50-100 in small bills.
- Paper road map of your region (GPS fails in dead zones).

## Seasonal Additions

=== "Winter Kit"
    - Ice scraper and snow brush.
    - Folding snow shovel.
    - Tire chains or traction mats (Sand Tracks, MAXSA).
    - Extra warm layers: insulated jacket, hat, gloves, wool socks.
    - Hand and body warmers (12-hour type).
    - Bag of sand or cat litter (40 lbs / 18 kg) for traction.
    - Candle and metal can (emergency heat source inside vehicle — crack window for ventilation).
    - Sleeping bag rated to 0 degF (-18 degC) or lower.

    !!! danger "WARNING"
        Never run the engine for heat with snow blocking the exhaust pipe. Carbon monoxide enters the cabin within minutes. Clear the tailpipe before idling and crack a downwind window.

=== "Desert / Summer Kit"
    - Extra water: 2 gallons (7.6 L) per person beyond baseline.
    - Electrolyte packets.
    - Sunshade for windshield.
    - Wide-brim hat and sunscreen.
    - Extra coolant, 1 gallon (3.8 L).
    - Light-colored tarp for shade shelter if stranded.

=== "Flood / Hurricane Region"
    - Window breaker mounted on dashboard (submerged doors are nearly impossible to open).
    - Waterproof dry bag for electronics and documents.
    - 50 feet (15 m) of rope.
    - PFD (personal flotation device) if in flood-prone areas.

## Recovery Gear

| Item | Purpose | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| Tow strap (20 ft / 6 m) | Vehicle-to-vehicle recovery | Rated to 1.5x vehicle weight |
| D-ring shackles (2) | Attach strap to frame | 3/4 inch (19 mm) minimum |
| Tire plug kit + inflator | Field tire repair | Practice before you need it |
| Shovel (folding) | Dig out stuck vehicle | Full-size preferred if space allows |
| Traction boards | Regain grip on mud, sand, snow | Place under drive wheels |
| Hi-lift jack | Off-road vehicle recovery | Requires training — dangerous if misused |

!!! warning "CAUTION"
    Attach tow straps to frame-mounted recovery points only. Never attach to bumpers, axles, or suspension components. A snapping strap under tension is lethal.

## Kit by Vehicle Type

=== "Car / Sedan"
    - Compact kit stored in trunk.
    - Collapsible shovel.
    - Compact jump starter (replaces bulky cables).
    - Space-efficient food and water storage.

=== "Truck / SUV"
    - Full-size spare tire.
    - Full recovery kit (strap, shackles, boards).
    - Larger water supply (3-5 gallons / 11-19 L).
    - Toolbox with expanded tool set.
    - Tow hitch and rated ball mount.

=== "Motorcycle"
    - Tire plug kit and mini pump (CO2 cartridge).
    - Compact multi-tool.
    - Space blanket and rain poncho rolled tight.
    - USB charger wired to battery.
    - Minimal first aid in a belt pouch or tank bag.
    - 1 liter water bottle.

## Organization and Storage

- Use a dedicated bag or box that stays in the vehicle. Do not scatter items in the trunk.
- Secure heavy items so they do not become projectiles in a crash.
- Place the most critical items (first aid, seatbelt cutter, flashlight) within reach of the driver.
- Keep seasonal items in a separate bag that you swap in and out.

## Maintenance Schedule

| Task | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| Check water and food expiration | Every 6 months |
| Test flashlight and battery bank | Every 6 months |
| Inspect first aid kit | Every 6 months |
| Verify tire pressure (spare) | Every 3 months |
| Check fire extinguisher gauge | Every 6 months |
| Swap seasonal additions | At season change |
| Full kit inventory | Annually |

## Common Mistakes

- **No kit at all.** Most drivers carry nothing beyond a spare tire. A $100 kit covers the basics.
- **Letting the fuel tank run near empty.** Keep the tank at least half full. Fuel is warmth, light, and mobility.
- **Storing water in direct sunlight.** Heat degrades plastic containers and breeds bacteria. Store under seats or in the trunk.
- **Tow strap attached to bumper.** Bumper covers are plastic. Use frame-mounted recovery points only.
- **No practice with recovery gear.** Practice using your jack, changing a tire, and deploying traction boards before you are stuck on a dark roadside.
- **Forgetting the spare tire.** Check spare tire pressure quarterly. A flat spare is not a spare.
- **One-season kit.** A summer kit does not survive winter. Swap seasonal gear at every equinox.

## Quick Reference

| Situation | First Actions | Key Kit Items |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|
| Flat tire | Pull safely off road, hazards on, triangles out | Jack, lug wrench, spare or plug kit |
| Dead battery | Hood up, connect jumper cables or jump starter | Jumper cables or lithium starter |
| Stranded in winter | Stay with vehicle, run engine 10 min/hr, signal | Blankets, warmers, candle, shovel |
| Stranded in heat | Stay in shade of vehicle, ration water, signal | Water, shade tarp, electrolytes |
| Accident | Check injuries, call 911, move to safety | First aid kit, flares, fire extinguisher |
| Stuck (mud/snow/sand) | Clear around tires, place traction aids | Shovel, traction boards, cat litter |
