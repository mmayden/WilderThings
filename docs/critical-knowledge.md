# Things You Would Not Think to Look Up

> The facts that change what you do, that most people do not know, and that you would
> never search for — because you do not know they exist.

## At a Glance

- Every other way into this guide needs you to already know what you are looking for.
- This page is for what you would never think to search.
- The bar is deliberately high: acting on common sense here makes it **worse**.
- Read it once before a trip, not during one.
- Each entry links to the guide that explains it properly.

## Why This Page Exists

The navigation, the tags index, and the checklists all assume you have a starting word.
You look up "water purification" because you know water needs purifying.

The dangerous gaps are the other kind — where you would not know to ask. Nobody searches
"is rabbit dangerous to eat", because rabbit is food. Nobody searches "which fish stay
poisonous after cooking", because cooking is what makes food safe. That is exactly why
those facts kill people.

Everything here meets one test: **the belief most people already hold makes the situation
worse.** Being unaware is not enough to earn a place — plenty of useful things belong in
the guides and not here. It has to be something you would actively get wrong.

## Getting Found

### Your phone can reach a satellite with no signal at all

If you have an iPhone 14 or later, a Pixel 9 or 10, or a recent Galaxy, **dial emergency
services even with zero bars.** The phone falls back to a satellite by itself when no
cell tower answers. There is no app to open and no procedure to remember. On iPhone it is
free for the life of the phone.

Most people carrying this capability do not know they have it, and will look at an empty
signal bar and conclude there is no way to call for help.

!!! warning "CAUTION: it will not work under trees"
    The link needs a clear view of the sky. A forest canopy, a narrow canyon, or a cave
    will defeat it — and those are exactly the places people get lost. **A failed attempt
    under cover is not proof your phone cannot do it.**

    Move to the most open ground you can safely reach — a clearing, a ridgeline, a gravel
    bar, the edge of a lake — marking your route as you go. Then hold still, follow the
    on-screen prompt telling you where to point, and give it minutes rather than seconds.

    Send early, while the battery is good. Do not save it as a last resort.

Full detail, including which models and what it does not replace, in
[Satellite SOS Built Into Your Phone](navigation/gps-and-electronics.md#satellite-sos-built-into-your-phone).
