# Master Source List

> Authoritative sources referenced throughout the WilderThings survival guide collection. Organized by type for easy lookup.

## Military Field Manuals

- **FM 21-76 / FM 3-05.70 — Survival.** US Department of the Army. The foundational military survival manual covering evasion, shelter, water, fire, food, navigation, and survival in all climate zones. Superseded by ATP 3-50.3 but remains widely referenced.
- **ATP 3-50.21 — Survival, Evasion, and Recovery.** US Department of the Army. Current doctrine for personnel recovery and survival operations in military contexts.
- **TCCC Guidelines (Tactical Combat Casualty Care).** Committee on Tactical Combat Casualty Care (CoTCCC), Joint Trauma System. Evidence-based trauma care protocol for combat and austere environments. Updated annually. Available at: jts.health.mil.
- **FM 4-25.11 — First Aid.** US Department of the Army. Military first-aid procedures for battlefield injuries including hemorrhage control, airway management, and shock treatment.
- **FM 3-97.6 — Mountain Operations.** US Department of the Army. Mountain warfare manual covering cold-weather survival, high-altitude medicine, and mountain navigation.
- **MCRP 3-02F — Survival, Evasion, and Recovery.** US Marine Corps. Marine Corps survival doctrine with emphasis on evasion and coastal/jungle environments.
- **STP 21-1-SMCT — Soldier's Manual of Common Tasks.** US Department of the Army. Includes fundamental survival, first aid, and navigation tasks required of all soldiers.

## Medical References

- **Wilderness Medical Society (WMS) Clinical Practice Guidelines.** Peer-reviewed, evidence-based guidelines for wilderness medicine topics including hypothermia, altitude illness, wound management, anaphylaxis, and lightning injuries. Published in Wilderness & Environmental Medicine journal. Available at: wms.org.
- **American Red Cross — First Aid/CPR/AED Participant's Manual.** Standard civilian first-aid training reference covering bleeding control, shock, burns, fractures, CPR, and AED use.
- **American Heart Association (AHA) — Guidelines for CPR and Emergency Cardiovascular Care.** Current evidence-based standards for CPR technique, compression rate, rescue breathing, and AED protocols. Updated every five years.
- **NAEMT PHTLS (Prehospital Trauma Life Support).** National Association of Emergency Medical Technicians. Textbook and course covering trauma assessment and treatment in prehospital settings.
- **WAFA/WEMT Curriculum — Wilderness First Aid / Wilderness EMT.** Standardized wilderness medical training curricula used by NOLS Wilderness Medicine, SOLO, and WMA.
- **Merck Manual — Professional and Consumer Versions.** Comprehensive medical reference for disease identification, symptoms, and treatment. Available at: merckmanuals.com.
- **Wilderness & Environmental Medicine (Journal).** Peer-reviewed journal of the Wilderness Medical Society publishing original research on wilderness injuries, environmental illness, and austere-environment care.

## Government Sources

- **NOAA (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration).** Weather forecasting, storm warnings, tide tables, and climate data. Available at: noaa.gov.
- **NWS (National Weather Service).** Real-time weather observations, forecasts, and severe weather alerts. Part of NOAA. Available at: weather.gov.
- **CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention).** Guidelines on waterborne illness, vector-borne disease, travel health, and emergency preparedness. Available at: cdc.gov.
- **EPA (Environmental Protection Agency) — Water Treatment Guidelines.** Standards for safe drinking water including turbidity limits, disinfection methods, and contaminant thresholds. Available at: epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water.
- **USGS (United States Geological Survey).** Topographic maps, geological data, and water resource information. Topographic maps available at: usgs.gov/programs/national-geospatial-program.
- **NPS (National Park Service).** Backcountry safety guidelines, Leave No Trace principles, and wildlife encounter protocols. Available at: nps.gov.
- **FEMA (Federal Emergency Management Agency).** Disaster preparedness guides, emergency planning resources, and community resilience frameworks. Available at: fema.gov.
- **COSPAS-SARSAT.** International satellite-based search and rescue distress alert system. Technical documentation on PLB and EPIRB operation. Available at: cospas-sarsat.int.

## Organizations

- **NOLS (National Outdoor Leadership School).** Wilderness skills education including leadership, navigation, and wilderness medicine certification (WAFA, WFR, WEMT). Available at: nols.edu.
- **WMA (Wilderness Medical Associates International).** Wilderness medicine training and certification programs. Develops curricula for WAFA and WFR courses. Available at: wildmed.com.
- **Boy Scouts of America (BSA).** Merit badge programs in wilderness survival, first aid, orienteering, and camping. Field manuals and handbooks provide foundational outdoor skills. Available at: scouting.org.
- **SOLO (Stonehearth Open Learning Opportunities).** Wilderness medicine education provider offering WFA, WFR, and WEMT courses. Available at: soloschools.com.
- **Leave No Trace Center for Outdoor Ethics.** Seven principles of outdoor ethics for minimizing environmental impact. Available at: lnt.org.
- **International Search and Rescue Advisory Group (INSARAG).** UN coordination body for urban search and rescue, publishing guidelines on disaster response methodology.
- **Mountain Rescue Association (MRA).** Standards for mountain rescue operations in North America. Available at: mra.org.

## Academic and Research Sources

- **Journal of Wilderness Medicine.** Early peer-reviewed journal (now merged into Wilderness & Environmental Medicine) covering wilderness injury and illness research.
- **Forgey, William. *Wilderness Medicine: Beyond First Aid* (7th edition).** Textbook used in wilderness medical training covering diagnosis and treatment in backcountry settings.
- **Gonzales, Laurence. *Deep Survival: Who Lives, Who Dies, and Why.*** Research-based analysis of survival psychology drawing on neuroscience, accident investigation, and case studies.
- **Ripley, Amanda. *The Unthinkable: Who Survives When Disaster Strikes and Why.*** Research into human behavior during disasters including the denial-deliberation-action model.
- **Leach, John. "Cognitive Paralysis in an Emergency: The Role of the Supervisory Attentional System." *Aviation, Space, and Environmental Medicine.*** Academic paper on the freeze response and why 75% of people become bewildered and unable to act in emergencies.
- **Giesbrecht, Gordon. Research publications on hypothermia, cold water immersion, and thermoregulation.** University of Manitoba. Key researcher in cold-exposure survival physiology.

## Key Textbooks

- **Auerbach, Paul. *Wilderness Medicine* (7th edition). Elsevier, 2017.** The definitive 2,500-page medical reference for wilderness and austere-environment medicine. Covers every injury, illness, and environmental hazard encountered in remote settings.
- **Wiseman, John "Lofty." *SAS Survival Handbook* (revised edition). William Morrow, 2014.** Comprehensive general survival manual by a former SAS instructor. Covers all environments, climates, and core survival skills.
- **Canterbury, Dave. *Bushcraft 101*. Adams Media, 2014.** Foundational bushcraft reference organized around the "5 Cs of Survivability." Practical and field-tested.
- **Lundin, Cody. *98.6 Degrees: The Art of Keeping Your Ass Alive*. Gibbs Smith, 2003.** Thermoregulation-focused survival guide emphasizing core body temperature as the central survival priority.
- **Hawke, Mykel. *Hawke's Green Beret Survival Manual*. Running Press, 2012.** Field-practical survival strategies from a career Special Forces officer.
- **Brown, Tom Jr. *Tom Brown's Field Guide to Wilderness Survival*. Berkley, 1983.** Primitive skills and nature awareness from one of America's most well-known trackers.
- **Angier, Bradford. *How to Stay Alive in the Woods*. Black Dog & Leventhal, 1956 (revised 2001).** Classic guide to wilderness self-sufficiency.
- **Elias, Thomas, and Peter Dykeman. *Edible Wild Plants: A North American Field Guide*. Sterling, 2009.** Photographic field guide to 200+ edible species with preparation details.
- **Foster, Steven, and James Duke. *Peterson Field Guide to Medicinal Plants and Herbs* (3rd edition). Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2014.** Standard field reference for medicinal plant identification in eastern and central North America.
- **US Department of the Army. *FM 21-76: Survival*. 1992 (reprinted by various publishers).** The foundational military survival field manual.
