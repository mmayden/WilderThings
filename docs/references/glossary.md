# Survival Glossary

> Comprehensive A-Z glossary of survival terminology. Use this as a quick-reference when encountering unfamiliar terms throughout the guides.

## A

**xABCDE (Primary Survey)** — The trauma assessment sequence: exsanguinating haemorrhage,
Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure. ATLS added the leading "x" in its
11th edition (2025), formally putting catastrophic bleeding before airway — the priority
the military MARCH protocol had already used for years. See
[Trauma and Triage](../medical/trauma-and-triage.md).

**Azimuth** — A compass bearing measured in degrees clockwise from true north (0-360 degrees). Used to navigate toward a target.

**AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness)** — Illness caused by rapid ascent to high altitude, characterized by headache, nausea, and fatigue. Onset typically above 8,000 feet (2,400 m).

## B

**Baseplate Compass** — A compass mounted on a transparent rectangular base with a direction-of-travel arrow, used for taking bearings from a map and navigating in the field.

**Batoning** — Striking the spine of a knife with a baton (a short, heavy stick) to split wood along its grain. Used to produce kindling and access dry inner wood.

**Attack Point** — A distinct feature near your objective that you navigate to loosely,
before making a short precise approach from it. Turns one long accurate leg into a long
rough one plus a short careful one. See [Terrain Association](../navigation/terrain-association.md).

**Backstop** — See Catching Feature.

**Bearing** — The direction from one point to another, expressed in degrees from north. A compass bearing accounts for magnetic declination.

**Bivouac (Bivy)** — A temporary, minimalist camp or shelter, often just a sleeping bag and a lightweight waterproof cover.

**Bug-Out Bag (BOB)** — A pre-packed emergency bag containing supplies for 72 hours of self-sufficiency during an evacuation. See also: EDC, INCH bag.

**Bushcraft** — The skill set of thriving in a wilderness environment using knowledge and minimal tools. Encompasses fire craft, shelter building, foraging, tracking, and tool making.

## C

**Catching Feature (Backstop)** — A large, unmistakable feature *beyond* your objective
that tells you when you have overshot: a road, river, lakeshore, or ridgeline. Identify
one before you start moving.

**Cairn** — A man-made stack of stones used as a trail marker or navigational reference in open terrain.

**Char Cloth** — Cotton or linen fabric that has been partially burned (pyrolyzed) in the absence of oxygen to create a material that catches sparks easily and holds an ember.

**Conduction** — Heat transfer through direct contact between objects. A major cause of heat loss when lying on cold ground without insulation.

**Convection** — Heat transfer through moving air or water across the body. Wind chill is a convection effect.

**CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)** — An emergency procedure combining chest compressions and rescue breaths to maintain circulation and oxygenation when the heart stops. Standard adult rate: 30 compressions to 2 breaths.

## D

**Dead Reckoning** — Navigating by tracking your direction and distance traveled from a known starting point, without external references.

**Debris Hut** — A small, low shelter built from a ridgepole propped against a support, covered with layers of leaves, branches, and forest debris for insulation. See [Debris Hut](../shelter/debris-hut.md).

**Declination** — The angular difference between magnetic north (where a compass needle points) and true north (geographic north). Varies by location and must be accounted for when navigating with map and compass.

**Dehydration** — A dangerous deficit of body water. Symptoms progress from thirst and dark urine to confusion, rapid heartbeat, and organ failure. A leading cause of death in survival situations.

## E

**EDC (Everyday Carry)** — The essential items a person carries daily for emergency preparedness: knife, lighter, flashlight, cordage, and similar tools.

**Edibility Test (Universal Edibility Test)** — A multi-step protocol for testing unknown
plants, involving skin contact, lip contact, tongue contact, chewing, and small ingestion
with 8-hour waits between steps. **It does not detect the plants most likely to kill
you** — several deadly species taste mild and act hours later, and delayed-onset toxins
pass the test entirely. It costs 24 hours and considerable water for a plant part worth
perhaps 15-30 calories. See [Foraging Basics](../food/foraging-basics.md).

**Ember** — A small, glowing piece of combustible material produced by friction fire methods or char cloth. Placed into a tinder bundle and blown into flame.

**Evaporation** — Heat loss caused by moisture turning to vapor on the skin surface. Wet clothing dramatically increases heat loss through this mechanism.

**Exposure** — Prolonged subjection to extreme environmental conditions (cold, heat, wind, rain) without adequate protection. A primary killer in wilderness emergencies.

## F

**Fatwood** — Resinous wood found in the stumps and heartwood of dead conifers, particularly pine. Extremely flammable and effective as fire-starting material even when wet.

**Ferro Rod (Ferrocerium Rod)** — A metallic fire-starting tool that produces a shower of sparks exceeding 5,000°F (2,760°C) when scraped with a hard edge. Works when wet.

**Fire Lay** — The arrangement of tinder, kindling, and fuel wood before ignition. Common types: teepee, log cabin, lean-to, star, and platform.

**Fire Triangle** — The three elements required for combustion: heat, fuel, and oxygen. Remove any one element and the fire goes out.

**Fording** — Crossing a body of water on foot. Requires assessment of depth, current speed, and footing.

**Foraging** — Gathering wild plants, fungi, insects, or other natural foods from the environment. See [Foraging Basics](../food/foraging-basics.md).

## G

**GOES (Geostationary Operational Environmental Satellite)** — A weather satellite system used by NOAA. Relevant to understanding weather forecasting tools and satellite-based communication.

**Ground-to-Air Signal** — A large visual marker laid out on the ground to communicate with search aircraft. Standard symbols include V (need assistance), X (need medical help), and an arrow (traveling this direction).

## H

**Handrail (Navigation)** — A linear terrain feature (river, ridge, road, fence line)
followed to maintain direction without constant compass work. Paired with an **attack
point** near the objective and a **catching feature** beyond it. See
[Terrain Association](../navigation/terrain-association.md).

**Hardtack** — A simple, long-lasting survival biscuit made from flour, water, and salt. Historical emergency ration.

**Hemostatic Agent** — A substance (such as QuikClot or Celox) applied to a wound to accelerate blood clotting and control severe hemorrhage.

**Hypothermia** — A dangerous drop in core body temperature below 95°F (35°C). Progresses through shivering, confusion, loss of coordination, and cardiac arrest. See [Hypothermia and Heatstroke](../medical/hypothermia-and-heatstroke.md).

**Heatstroke** — A life-threatening condition in which the body's core temperature rises above 104°F (40°C) and the thermoregulation system fails. Requires immediate aggressive cooling. See [Hypothermia and Heatstroke](../medical/hypothermia-and-heatstroke.md).

## I

**INCH Bag (I'm Never Coming Home)** — A long-term survival pack designed for indefinite self-sufficiency, as opposed to a 72-hour bug-out bag.

**Insulation** — Material that traps dead air to slow heat transfer. In shelters, provided by leaves, grass, boughs, or snow. In clothing, provided by down, wool, or synthetic fill.

## J

**Jerky** — Lean meat that has been trimmed, sliced thin, and dried to prevent spoilage. A critical food-preservation technique in wilderness survival.

## K

**Kindling** — Small, dry sticks and twigs (pencil-thickness or smaller) placed over tinder to catch fire and build heat for larger fuel wood. See [Fire Principles](../fire/fire-principles.md).

**Knot (Bowline)** — A fixed-loop knot that does not slip under load. One of the most essential survival knots. See [Knots and Lashing](../tools-and-craft/knots-and-lashing.md).

## L

**Lean-To** — A simple shelter constructed by leaning branches or a tarp against a horizontal support, creating a single sloped wall for wind and rain protection.

**Lensatic Compass** — A military-style compass with a sighting lens and wire for precise bearing measurement. Standard issue in US military (e.g., Cammenga 3H).

**LNT (Leave No Trace)** — Seven principles for minimizing environmental impact during outdoor activities: plan ahead, travel on durable surfaces, dispose of waste properly, leave what you find, minimize campfire impacts, respect wildlife, be considerate of others.

## M

**MARCH (Massive hemorrhage, Airway, Respiration, Circulation, Hypothermia)** — A tactical trauma assessment protocol used in TCCC (Tactical Combat Casualty Care). Prioritizes life threats in combat or austere environments.

**Morse Code (SOS)** — The international distress signal: three short, three long, three short (... --- ...). Transmitted by light, sound, or radio.

## N

**NPA (Nasopharyngeal Airway)** — A flexible tube inserted through the nostril to maintain an open airway in a semiconscious patient. Tolerated better than oral airways.

## O

**Orienteering** — The skill of navigating between points using a map and compass. Fundamental to wilderness travel and search-and-rescue.

## P

**PLB (Personal Locator Beacon)** — A device that transmits a distress signal on 406 MHz to the COSPAS-SARSAT satellite system, alerting rescue authorities to your GPS position.

**Potable** — Safe to drink. Water is potable when it has been treated to remove or kill pathogens, chemical contaminants, and particulates.

**Pressure Dressing** — A bandage applied with firm pressure to a wound to control bleeding. Maintains direct pressure without requiring someone to hold it.

**Primitive Fire** — Fire created without modern ignition tools, using methods such as bow drill, hand drill, fire plow, or fire saw. All need many hours of practice to work reliably and are not a realistic first resort when stranded. See [Spark and Lens Methods](../fire/spark-methods.md).

**Prusik Knot** — A friction hitch that grips a rope when weighted and slides freely when unweighted. Used for ascending ropes, tensioning ridgelines, and self-rescue.

## Q

**Quarantine (Water)** — The practice of isolating collected water and allowing sediment to settle before filtering or treating. Improves the effectiveness of purification methods.

## R

**Radiation (Heat)** — Heat loss through infrared emission from the body to cooler surroundings. Occurs without contact or air movement. A reflective survival blanket counters this.

**Ridgepole** — The central, horizontal support beam of a shelter (especially a debris hut or A-frame), running from the raised front opening to the ground at the rear. See [Shelter Principles](../shelter/shelter-principles.md).

**Rule of Threes** — A survival prioritization framework: 3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter (in harsh conditions), 3 days without water, 3 weeks without food.

## S

**Shelter-in-Place** — Remaining in your current location and fortifying it rather than traveling. Appropriate when travel is more dangerous than staying put.

**Signal Mirror** — A reflective device used to flash sunlight toward aircraft or distant rescuers. Effective range exceeds 10 miles (16 km) in clear conditions. See [Signaling for Rescue](../navigation/signaling-for-rescue.md).

**Snare** — A trap using a loop of wire or cordage to capture small game by tightening around the animal as it moves through the loop. See [Trapping and Snares](../food/trapping-and-snares.md).

**SODIS (Solar Water Disinfection)** — A water purification method using UV radiation from sunlight. Clear water in a transparent PET bottle is exposed to direct sunlight for at least 6 hours (or 2 days under overcast skies). See [Purification](../water/purification.md).

**Spindle** — The vertical, rotating component of a friction fire set (bow drill or hand drill) that presses into the fireboard to create heat through friction.

**Splint** — A rigid or semi-rigid support applied to immobilize a fractured or dislocated bone. Constructed from sticks, SAM splints, or improvised materials padded with cloth. See [Fractures and Splints](../medical/fractures-and-splints.md).

**STOP Protocol** — A survival mnemonic: Stop (do not panic), Think (assess the situation), Observe (inventory resources and surroundings), Plan (decide on a course of action). See [Survival Mindset](../psychology/survival-mindset.md).

## T

**TCCC (Tactical Combat Casualty Care)** — A set of evidence-based trauma care guidelines developed by the US military for use in combat and austere environments. Emphasizes hemorrhage control and hypothermia prevention.

**Tinder** — The finest, most combustible material in a fire lay. Must catch a spark or ember and sustain flame long enough to ignite kindling. Examples: dry grass, birch bark, cedar shavings, char cloth. See [Fire Principles](../fire/fire-principles.md).

**Tinder Bundle** — A bird's-nest-shaped collection of fine, dry tinder material into which an ember is placed and blown into flame.

**Tourniquet** — A constricting device applied to a limb to cut off arterial blood flow
and stop life-threatening haemorrhage. Applied 2-3 inches (5-7.5 cm) above the wound and
tightened until bleeding stops. Note the time. Current guidance asks for reassessment at
two hours; never slacken one periodically to restore circulation. See
[First Aid Basics](../medical/first-aid-basics.md#how-long-a-tourniquet-can-stay-on).

**Triage** — The process of sorting casualties by severity to allocate treatment resources. Categories: immediate, delayed, minimal, expectant. See [Trauma and Triage](../medical/trauma-and-triage.md).

**Turbidity** — The cloudiness or haziness of water caused by suspended particles. High turbidity reduces the effectiveness of UV and chemical purification methods. Pre-filter turbid water before treatment.

## U

**Universal Edibility Test** — See Edibility Test.

**UV Purification** — Water treatment using ultraviolet light to destroy the DNA of pathogens, rendering them unable to reproduce. Devices like SteriPEN deliver a measured UV dose. Requires low-turbidity water.

## V

**Vapor Barrier** — A waterproof layer (plastic sheet, trash bag) placed close to the body inside insulation to prevent moisture from saturating insulating layers. Used in extreme cold.

## W

**WBGT (Wet Bulb Globe Temperature)** — An environmental heat stress index that accounts for temperature, humidity, wind, and solar radiation. Used to assess risk of heatstroke.

**Wicking** — The movement of moisture away from the skin through capillary action in fabric. Base layers made of wool or synthetic materials wick sweat to keep the skin dry.

**Windbreak** — A barrier (natural or constructed) that blocks or deflects wind to reduce convective heat loss at a camp or shelter site.

**Wound Packing** — The technique of filling a deep wound cavity with hemostatic gauze or plain gauze, pressing firmly to apply direct pressure to the bleeding source from within.

## X

**XSTAT** — A hemostatic device consisting of small, compressed sponges injected into a wound cavity where they expand to apply internal pressure and stop bleeding. Military and specialized first-aid use.

## Y

**Yucca** — A genus of plants found in arid environments with strong leaf fibers used for cordage and roots that produce a soapy lather for cleaning.

## Z

**Z-Fold Gauze** — Gauze that is folded in a zigzag pattern for rapid deployment in wound packing. Standard component of trauma first-aid kits (e.g., IFAK).
