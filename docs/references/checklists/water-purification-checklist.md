# Water Purification Checklist

> Use this checklist whenever you need to make water safe to drink in the field. Untreated water can carry bacteria, viruses, and parasites that cause severe illness. Always treat water before drinking.

## Checklist

### Source Assessment

- [ ] Locate a water source: stream, river, lake, spring, rainwater, snow, or dew
- [ ] Prefer moving water over still water (streams over ponds)
- [ ] Prefer clear water over murky water
- [ ] Collect water upstream of any camps, trails, animal activity, or agriculture
- [ ] Avoid water near mining operations, industrial sites, or chemical runoff (treatment does not remove chemical contaminants)
- [ ] Use animal tracks to help you *find* water — but never as evidence it is safe. Animals drink water full of Giardia and Cryptosporidium, and are usually the source of it
- [ ] See [Finding Water](../../water/finding-water.md) for detailed source identification

### Pre-Filtering

- [ ] Allow collected water to sit undisturbed for 30 minutes so sediment settles to the bottom
- [ ] Pour or decant the clearer water off the top into a separate container
- [ ] If the water is still turbid, filter through layered material:
  - Layer 1 (bottom): Fine sand or crushed charcoal
  - Layer 2 (middle): Gravel or small pebbles
  - Layer 3 (top): Grass or cloth
- [ ] Alternatively, filter through a clean cloth (bandana, shirt) folded several times
- [ ] Pre-filtering improves the effectiveness of all purification methods and extends filter cartridge life

### Method Selection

Choose the best available method based on your equipment and situation:

- [ ] **Boiling** — Most reliable method. Effective against all pathogens. Requires fire and a container
- [ ] **Chemical treatment (iodine or chlorine dioxide)** — Lightweight and simple. Requires clear water and wait time
- [ ] **Pump or gravity filter** — Removes bacteria and protozoa. May not remove viruses (check filter rating)
- [ ] **UV treatment (SteriPEN or SODIS)** — Effective against all pathogens. Requires clear water and either batteries or sunlight
- [ ] **Combination** — Use two methods for maximum safety (e.g., filter then chemical treat)

### Treatment Steps

#### Boiling

- [ ] Bring water to a rolling boil
- [ ] Maintain the rolling boil for 1 minute at elevations below 6,500 feet (2,000 m)
- [ ] Maintain the rolling boil for 3 minutes at elevations above 6,500 feet (2,000 m)
- [ ] Allow to cool before drinking. Do not add untreated water or ice

#### Chemical Treatment

- [ ] Follow the manufacturer's dosage instructions exactly
- [ ] **Iodine tablets:** Add tablets per instructions (typically 1-2 tablets per quart/liter). Wait 30 minutes. In cold or cloudy water, double the wait time or dosage
- [ ] **Chlorine dioxide (Aquamira, Katadyn Micropur).** Mix components per instructions. Add to water. Wait 15-30 minutes (4 hours for Cryptosporidium)
- [ ] **Household bleach (sodium hypochlorite 5-8%).** Add 2 drops per quart (liter) of clear water, or 4 drops if water is cloudy. Wait 30 minutes. Water should have a slight chlorine smell; if not, repeat the dose and wait another 30 minutes

#### Filtration

- [ ] Assemble the filter per manufacturer instructions
- [ ] Prime the filter if required
- [ ] Pump or pour water through the filter into a clean container
- [ ] Ensure the clean container has not been contaminated by untreated water
- [ ] Check the filter rating: 0.2 micron removes bacteria and protozoa; a purifier rated to 0.02 micron also removes viruses

#### UV Treatment

- [ ] **SteriPEN:** Insert into clear water. Activate and stir for the prescribed time (typically 90 seconds per liter). Ensure the entire volume is exposed
- [ ] **SODIS (Solar Disinfection):** Fill a clear PET plastic bottle (2 liters or smaller) with clear water. Lay the bottle on a reflective surface (metal sheet, light-colored rock) in direct sunlight. Expose for at least 6 hours on a sunny day or 2 full days under overcast skies

### Storage

- [ ] Store treated water in clean, covered containers
- [ ] Do not mix treated and untreated water
- [ ] Label or designate containers clearly: mark "TREATED" or "RAW"
- [ ] Keep containers out of direct sunlight to prevent algae growth in stored water
- [ ] If stored for more than 24 hours, re-treat before drinking

## Notes

- Dehydration kills faster than most waterborne illnesses. In an extreme emergency with no purification method available, drinking untreated water is preferable to severe dehydration. Plan to treat for illness later.
- Snow and ice must be melted and then treated. Eating snow directly lowers core body temperature and accelerates hypothermia.
- Boiling does not remove chemical contaminants, heavy metals, or sediment. Pre-filter turbid water before boiling.
- Iodine is not recommended for pregnant women or people with thyroid conditions.

!!! warning "CAUTION"
    Never assume water is safe because it looks clear. Many pathogens are invisible. Always treat before drinking.
