# Water Purification

> Kill pathogens and remove contaminants from wild water using heat, chemicals, UV light, and filtration.

## At a Glance

- Boiling is the most reliable field method — a rolling boil for 1 minute at sea level, 3 minutes above 6,500 feet (2,000 m).
- Chemical treatment works but requires wait time and does not remove particulates or chemical pollutants.
- UV treatment kills pathogens but only works in clear water.
- Filtration removes protozoa and bacteria; most field filters do NOT remove viruses.
- Pre-filter all turbid water before applying any purification method.

## Understanding the Threats

### What Lives in Untreated Water

| Pathogen | Size | Examples | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protozoa | 1–300 microns | Giardia, Cryptosporidium | Diarrhea, cramps, 1–2 week onset |
| Bacteria | 0.1–10 microns | E. coli, Salmonella, Cholera | Diarrhea, vomiting, hours–days onset |
| Viruses | 0.005–0.1 microns | Hepatitis A, Norovirus, Rotavirus | Vomiting, fever, days–weeks onset |

### Non-Biological Contaminants

- **Heavy metals** (lead, mercury, arsenic) — no field method removes these reliably.
- **Pesticides and herbicides** — activated carbon filters reduce some; boiling and chemicals do not remove them.
- **Dissolved minerals** — generally harmless but may cause taste issues.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    No single field purification method removes all threats. Understand what each method does and does not address.

## Boiling

Boiling is the gold standard. Heat kills all waterborne pathogens — protozoa, bacteria, and viruses — without chemicals or special equipment.

### Procedure

1. Pre-filter turbid water through cloth, sand, or a bandana to remove sediment.
2. Bring water to a **full rolling boil** (vigorous, sustained bubbling).
3. Maintain the rolling boil for the required time (see below).
4. Remove from heat and let cool. Do not add untreated water or ice to cool it.
5. Store in a clean, covered container.

### Boil Times by Altitude

| Altitude | Boil Time | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Sea level to 6,500 ft (0–2,000 m) | 1 minute | Water boils at 212 F / 100 C — sufficient for all pathogens |
| 6,500–13,000 ft (2,000–4,000 m) | 3 minutes | Boiling point drops to ~194 F / 90 C — longer exposure needed |
| Above 13,000 ft (4,000 m) | 5 minutes | Boiling point drops further — extend time for safety margin |

!!! danger "WARNING"
    At extreme altitude, water boils at significantly lower temperatures. Increasing boil time compensates for the reduced temperature.

### What Boiling Removes

- All protozoa (including Cryptosporidium cysts).
- All bacteria.
- All viruses.

### What Boiling Does NOT Remove

- Chemical contaminants (pesticides, heavy metals, fuel).
- Sediment and particulates (pre-filter first).
- Dissolved salts.
- Taste and odor from organic matter.

### Tips

- If you cannot measure time, a vigorous rolling boil visible from several feet away is a reliable indicator — by the time water reaches a full boil, most pathogens are already dead.
- A metal container is ideal. In an emergency, boil water in a plastic bottle by suspending it above coals (keep the bottle full — water absorbs heat before the plastic melts). A bamboo section or birch bark vessel also works over fire.
- Boiled water tastes flat because dissolved oxygen escapes. Pour the water back and forth between two containers to re-aerate it.

## Chemical Treatment

### Iodine Tablets

Iodine is a proven disinfectant effective against bacteria, viruses, and most protozoa.

**Procedure:**

1. Pre-filter turbid water.
2. Add tablets per manufacturer directions — typically 1–2 tablets per 1 quart / 1 liter.
3. Wait **30 minutes** in warm water (above 68 F / 20 C).
4. Wait **60 minutes** in cold water (below 68 F / 20 C) or if water is cloudy.
5. Shake the container and slightly loosen the cap to allow treated water to rinse the threads.

**Effective against:** bacteria, viruses, Giardia.

**NOT effective against:** Cryptosporidium (iodine does not reliably kill Crypto cysts at field concentrations).

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Do not use iodine treatment for more than 3 consecutive weeks. Prolonged iodine ingestion affects thyroid function. Pregnant individuals and those with thyroid conditions must avoid iodine treatment entirely.

### Chlorine Dioxide (e.g., Aquamira, Katadyn Micropur)

Chlorine dioxide is more effective than iodine and kills Cryptosporidium with extended contact time.

**Procedure:**

1. Pre-filter turbid water.
2. Mix components per manufacturer directions (typically Part A and Part B, wait 5 minutes for activation).
3. Add to water — standard dose is 7 drops each of Part A and Part B per 1 quart / 1 liter.
4. Wait **15 minutes** for bacteria and viruses.
5. Wait **4 hours** for Cryptosporidium.
6. Rinse container threads with treated water.

**Effective against:** bacteria, viruses, Giardia, and Cryptosporidium (with 4-hour wait).

**NOT effective against:** chemical contaminants, heavy metals, sediment.

### Household Bleach (Sodium Hypochlorite)

Use **unscented liquid household bleach** with 5.25–8.25% sodium hypochlorite.

**Procedure:**

1. Pre-filter turbid water.
2. Add bleach using the following ratios:

| Water Volume | Bleach (5.25–6%) | Bleach (8.25%) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 quart / 1 liter | 2 drops | 2 drops |
| 1 gallon / 4 liters | 8 drops (1/8 tsp) | 6 drops |
| 5 gallons / 19 liters | 40 drops (5/8 tsp) | 30 drops (1/2 tsp) |

3. Stir and wait **30 minutes**.
4. The water should have a slight chlorine smell after treatment. If it does not, repeat the dose and wait another 30 minutes.
5. If water is very cold or cloudy, **double the dose** and **double the wait time**.

**Effective against:** bacteria, viruses, Giardia.

**NOT effective against:** Cryptosporidium at standard doses.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Use only plain, unscented bleach. Scented, color-safe, or "splashless" bleach contains additives that are toxic. Check the label — it must list sodium hypochlorite as the sole active ingredient.

### Chemical Treatment Summary

| Chemical | Kills Crypto | Wait Time | Shelf Life |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iodine tablets | **No** | 30–60 min | 4–5 years sealed |
| Chlorine dioxide | Yes (4 hr wait) | 15 min–4 hr | 4 years sealed |
| Bleach (5.25%) | **No** | 30–60 min | 6–12 months |

## UV Treatment

Ultraviolet light at 254 nm wavelength destroys the DNA of pathogens, rendering them unable to reproduce.

### SteriPEN and Similar Devices

**Procedure:**

1. Pre-filter water until **clear** — UV cannot penetrate turbid water.
2. Insert the UV lamp into the container (typically 0.5–1 quart / 0.5–1 liter).
3. Activate and stir for the prescribed time (usually 48–90 seconds per treatment cycle).
4. Treat and drink. No wait time after treatment.

**Effective against:** bacteria, viruses, protozoa (including Cryptosporidium).

**NOT effective against:** chemical contaminants, particulates.

**Limitations:**

- Requires batteries or charging — plan for power failure.
- Effective **only in clear water** — particles shield pathogens from UV exposure.
- Does not improve taste or remove sediment.
- Glass and some plastic containers block UV; use only compatible containers.

### SODIS (Solar Water Disinfection)

SODIS uses the sun's natural UV-A radiation and heat to kill pathogens in clear PET plastic bottles.

**Procedure:**

1. Obtain a clear PET plastic bottle (check for the "1" recycling symbol on the bottom). Bottles up to 2 liters work. Do not use colored, opaque, or PVC bottles.
2. Pre-filter water until clear. SODIS does not work in turbid water.
3. Fill the bottle three-quarters full, cap it, and **shake vigorously for 20 seconds** to oxygenate the water. Then fill completely and recap.
4. Place the bottle on its side in direct sunlight — a reflective surface (corrugated metal roof, aluminum foil, light-colored rock) underneath doubles UV exposure.
5. Expose for **6 hours in full sun** or **2 full days in overcast conditions**.
6. Water is ready to drink after the exposure period.

**Effective against:** bacteria, viruses, protozoa.

**NOT effective against:** chemical contaminants, spore-forming bacteria in heavily contaminated water.

**Optimizations:**

- Paint one side of the bottle black — the dark side faces down on the reflective surface, increasing heat absorption. The clear side faces up for UV penetration.
- If water temperature reaches 122 F / 50 C during exposure, the combined UV and thermal effect reduces the required time to **1 hour**.
- Use multiple bottles simultaneously for continuous supply.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    SODIS is ineffective in turbid water. If you cannot read newspaper print through the bottle, the water is too cloudy — pre-filter first.

## Filtration

### Commercial Filters

Commercial pump, gravity, and squeeze filters use ceramic, hollow fiber, or activated carbon elements to physically remove pathogens.

| Filter Type | Removes Viruses | Removes Chemicals | Flow Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hollow fiber (0.1–0.2 micron) | **No** | **No** | Fast |
| Ceramic (0.2–0.5 micron) | **No** | **No** | Slow |
| Activated carbon + hollow fiber | **No** | Some (taste, chlorine) | Medium |
| Purifier (0.02 micron or chemical element) | Yes | Some | Slow |

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Most field filters do NOT remove viruses. In areas with suspected human waste contamination (developing regions, downstream of settlements), combine filtration with chemical treatment or boiling.

### Popular Commercial Options

- **Sawyer Squeeze / Mini** — hollow fiber, 0.1 micron. Lightweight, backflush-capable. No virus removal.
- **Katadyn Pocket** — ceramic, 0.2 micron. Heavy but extremely durable. Military-grade longevity.
- **MSR Guardian** — hollow fiber purifier, 0.02 micron. Removes viruses. Self-cleaning. Expensive.
- **Gravity systems (Platypus, Sawyer)** — hang and wait. No pumping effort. Ideal for camp use.

### Improvised Filters

When no commercial filter is available, build a layered filter to remove particulates and some pathogens.

**Tripod Filter Construction:**

1. Use a container with a hole in the bottom (sock, pant leg tied at the ankle, plastic bottle with bottom cut off).
2. Layer the following materials from bottom to top:
   - **Layer 1 (bottom):** Small gravel or pebbles — 2 inches / 5 cm.
   - **Layer 2:** Coarse sand — 2 inches / 5 cm.
   - **Layer 3:** Fine sand — 4 inches / 10 cm.
   - **Layer 4:** Crushed charcoal from a hardwood fire — 4 inches / 10 cm. Use **only** charcoal you made yourself; never commercial charcoal briquettes, which contain binders, accelerants, and fillers that leach into the water.
   - **Layer 5:** Fine sand — 2 inches / 5 cm.
   - **Layer 6 (top):** Gravel — 2 inches / 5 cm.
3. Pour water through the top. Collect filtered water at the bottom.
4. Discard the first two passes — they flush loose material.
5. **Always purify** filtered water by boiling or chemical treatment afterward.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Improvised filters reduce sediment and improve taste but do NOT reliably remove bacteria, viruses, or protozoa. Always follow improvised filtration with boiling or chemical treatment.

### Charcoal Production for Filters

1. Burn hardwood (oak, maple, hickory) in a hot fire until you have glowing coals.
2. Remove coals and smother them in a sealed container (tin can with lid, hole covered with dirt) to extinguish without ash formation.
3. Once cool, crush the charcoal into small pieces (pea-sized to powder).
4. Crushed charcoal adsorbs organic chemicals, reduces odor, and improves taste.

## Gravity Filters

Gravity filters require no pumping or squeezing — hang the dirty water bag above the clean bag and let gravity do the work.

### Setup

1. Fill the "dirty" reservoir with pre-filtered water.
2. Hang it at least 3 feet (1 m) above the "clean" collection container.
3. Connect the filter element between the two bags via tubing.
4. Open the valve and let water flow. A full liter typically takes 2–5 minutes depending on the filter.

### Advantages

- Hands-free operation.
- Ideal for group use — filter while performing other tasks.
- Less physical effort than pump filters.

### Disadvantages

- Requires hanging infrastructure (tree branch, ridgepole).
- Slower than pump filters when only small quantities are needed.
- Filter elements clog faster with turbid water — pre-filter always.

## Pre-Filtering Turbid Water

All purification methods work better — and some only work — with clear water. Pre-filter before treating.

### Methods

1. **Cloth filter** — pour water through a tightly woven bandana, t-shirt, or coffee filter. Removes large particulates and some organisms.
2. **Settle and decant** — fill a container with turbid water, let it sit undisturbed for 2–4 hours, then carefully pour off the clear top layer without disturbing the settled sediment.
3. **Sand pre-filter** — pour water through a container filled with clean sand. Captures fine sediment.
4. **Alum (potassium aluminum sulfate)** — add a pinch to a gallon (4 L) of turbid water, stir for 5 minutes, and wait 30 minutes. Suspended particles clump together and settle. Decant the clear water.

### When to Pre-Filter

- Before UV treatment (SODIS or SteriPEN) — always. UV fails in cloudy water.
- Before chemical treatment — reduces required dose and wait time.
- Before commercial filters — extends filter life significantly.
- Before boiling — optional but improves taste and reduces sediment in the pot.

## Choosing the Right Method

| Situation | Best Method | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Have fire and metal container | Boiling | Most reliable, kills everything |
| No fire, have chemicals | Chemical treatment | Lightweight, effective |
| No fire, no chemicals, clear water | SODIS | Free, effective with patience |
| Group setting at camp | Gravity filter + chemical | Hands-free, high volume |
| Backpacking with reliable gear | Hollow fiber filter + chemical backup | Fast, lightweight |
| Unknown contamination, possible viruses | Boil or purifier (0.02 micron) | Only methods addressing all pathogens |
| Emergency, no supplies | Improvised filter + boiling | Last resort combo |

## Combining Methods for Maximum Safety

For the safest water in the field, combine methods:

1. **Pre-filter** through cloth or sand to remove particulates.
2. **Filter** through a commercial or improvised filter to remove protozoa and bacteria.
3. **Chemically treat or boil** to kill viruses and any pathogens that passed the filter.

This layered approach addresses the full spectrum of threats.

## Storage After Treatment

- Store purified water in a clean, sealed container.
- Do not dip untreated hands or utensils into purified water.
- Chemically treated water maintains residual disinfection — water treated with chlorine or iodine resists recontamination for hours.
- Boiled and UV-treated water has **no residual protection** — recontamination is possible immediately. Treat storage containers carefully.
- Label or separate treated water from untreated water.

## Common Mistakes

- **Assuming clear water is clean** — crystal-clear mountain streams carry Giardia and bacteria. Purify everything.
- **Skipping pre-filtration** — turbid water reduces the effectiveness of every purification method. Filter first.
- **Insufficient chemical contact time** — rushing chemical treatment leaves pathogens alive. Wait the full duration.
- **Using expired chemicals** — iodine tablets lose potency once the bottle is opened. Replace annually.
- **Relying solely on filtration in high-risk areas** — most filters miss viruses. Add chemical treatment near human settlements.
- **Using SODIS with scratched or old PET bottles** — scratches block UV penetration. Use clear, undamaged bottles.
- **Forgetting thread contamination** — after chemical treatment, tilt the container to let treated water rinse the cap threads before drinking.
- **Ignoring recontamination** — dipping a dirty cup into purified water reintroduces pathogens. Use a pour spout.
