# Bear Safety and Encounter Protocol

> Identify the bear, prevent the encounter, survive the confrontation.

## At a Glance

- Black bears and grizzlies demand opposite responses — misidentification kills.
- Bear spray is more effective than firearms in stopping a charge (92% vs 67% success rate).
- Store all food, toiletries, and scented items at least 200 ft (60 m) from your sleeping area.
- A mother with cubs is the single most dangerous scenario in bear country.
- Most bear encounters end without contact if you respond correctly in the first 5 seconds.

## Identification

### Black Bear

- **Size:** 200–400 lb (90–180 kg); up to 600 lb (270 kg) in exceptional cases.
- **Shoulder:** No pronounced hump.
- **Face profile:** Straight or slightly convex from forehead to nose ("Roman nose").
- **Ears:** Tall, pointed, prominent relative to head size.
- **Claws:** Short, curved, 1.5 in (4 cm). Dark colored. Designed for climbing.
- **Tracks:** Front track about 4.5 in (11 cm) long. Toes tightly spaced in an arc.
- **Color:** Misleading. Ranges from blonde to cinnamon to jet black. Do NOT rely on color alone.

### Grizzly / Brown Bear

- **Size:** 400–800 lb (180–360 kg); coastal brown bears exceed 1,000 lb (450 kg).
- **Shoulder:** Prominent muscular hump above the shoulders. This is the defining field mark.
- **Face profile:** Concave, dish-shaped. Forehead dips before the nose.
- **Ears:** Short, rounded, appear small relative to the massive head.
- **Claws:** Long, slightly curved, 2–4 in (5–10 cm). Light colored. Designed for digging.
- **Tracks:** Front track about 5–7 in (13–18 cm) long. Toes more widely spaced and less arced.
- **Color:** Varies from blonde to dark brown. Guard hairs often silver-tipped ("grizzled").

### Track Analysis

Measure the front track width. Under 4 in (10 cm) is almost certainly black bear. Over 5 in (13 cm) is almost certainly grizzly. Between 4–5 in (10–13 cm), look at the toe arrangement: draw a straight line across the tops of the outer toes. In a black bear track, the line crosses through the middle toes. In a grizzly track, the middle toes fall below the line.

## Prevention

### Food Storage

Use one of three approved methods:

1. **Bear canister:** Required in many wilderness areas. Place it on flat ground at least 200 ft (60 m) from camp. Do NOT place near cliffs or water where a bear could bat it out of reach.
2. **Bear hang (PCT method):** Use a stuff sack, 50 ft (15 m) of paracord, and a carabiner. Hang food at least 12 ft (3.7 m) off the ground, 6 ft (1.8 m) from the trunk, and 6 ft (1.8 m) below the branch.
3. **Bear box/locker:** Use established metal food lockers at designated campsites. Close and latch them fully.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    "Odor-proof" bags reduce smell but do NOT eliminate it. Use them inside a canister or hang, never as a standalone method.

### Scent Discipline

- Cook and eat at least 200 ft (60 m) from your tent, preferably downwind.
- Never sleep in clothing you cooked in.
- Store toothpaste, sunscreen, lip balm, insect repellent, and menstrual products with your food.
- Strain dishwater and pack out solids. Broadcast strained water at least 200 ft (60 m) from camp.
- Do NOT bury food scraps. Bears will dig them up.

### Trail Behavior

- Make noise: clap, talk loudly, call out "Hey bear!" before blind corners and dense brush.
- Travel in groups of three or more. Groups of four or more have a near-zero attack rate.
- Stay on established trails when possible.
- Avoid hiking at dawn, dusk, or night in active bear areas.
- Watch for fresh sign: scat, tracks, digging, torn-apart logs, claw marks on trees.
- If you find a large animal carcass, leave the area immediately. Bears cache kills and defend them.

## Encounter Protocol

### Step 1: Identify the Bear

Before anything else, determine whether you face a black bear or a grizzly. Your survival response depends entirely on this identification.

### Step 2: Assess the Behavior

**Defensive behavior** (the bear feels threatened):
- Huffing, jaw-popping, swatting the ground.
- Ears back, head low.
- Bluff charges (stops short, veers away).
- Common near cubs, a food source, or when surprised.

**Predatory behavior** (the bear views you as food):
- Quiet, deliberate approach.
- Follows you persistently.
- Ears forward, head up, staring fixedly.
- Circles to get downwind of you.
- Approaches at night.

### Step 3: Respond

#### Black Bear — Any Behavior

- Stand your ground. Do NOT play dead.
- Make yourself as large as possible. Raise your arms, open your jacket wide.
- Shout, bang pots, throw rocks.
- If it makes contact, **fight back**. Target the nose and eyes.
- Black bears that attack are almost always treating you as prey. Playing dead guarantees injury.

#### Grizzly — Defensive Attack

- Deploy bear spray at 20–30 ft (6–9 m).
- If contact is made, **play dead**. Drop face-down, spread your legs for stability, lace your fingers behind your neck.
- Keep your pack on — it protects your back.
- Remain motionless until you are certain the bear has left. Wait at least 20 minutes.
- If the attack continues beyond 2 minutes, the bear may have switched to predatory behavior. Start fighting back.

#### Grizzly — Predatory Attack

- Do NOT play dead.
- Fight with everything available. Target the eyes and nose.
- Use rocks, sticks, trekking poles, knives.
- This is a fight for your life.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    If a grizzly enters your tent at night, it is a predatory attack regardless of circumstances. Fight back immediately.

## Bear Spray

### Selection

- Choose EPA-registered bear spray with a minimum of 2% capsaicin and related capsaicinoids (CRC).
- Minimum canister size: 7.9 oz (225 g). Larger is better.
- Minimum spray range: 16 ft (5 m). Most quality sprays reach 25–35 ft (8–11 m).
- Check the expiration date before every trip.

### Deployment

1. Remove the safety clip with your thumb.
2. Grip with both hands. Hold the canister slightly below eye level.
3. Aim at a downward angle toward the bear's face, just above ground level. Do NOT aim high.
4. Fire a 1–2 second burst when the bear is within 30 ft (9 m).
5. Adjust aim based on the spray cloud. Fire additional bursts as needed.
6. Create a wall of spray between you and the bear, then back away.
7. If the wind is at your back, spray freely. If the wind is in your face, wait until the bear is closer (15 ft / 4.5 m) and fire anyway — partial exposure still deters.

### Carry Position

Keep bear spray in a hip holster or chest harness. Never bury it in your pack. Practice drawing and removing the safety at least 10 times before entering bear country.

## Mother with Cubs

- Never position yourself between a mother and her cubs.
- If you see cubs, immediately scan for the mother. She is close.
- Speak in calm, low tones and back away slowly.
- Do NOT approach cubs, even if they appear orphaned. The mother is likely watching.
- If a mother charges, this is almost always defensive. Stand your ground, deploy bear spray. If she makes contact and she is a grizzly, play dead.

## Night Encounters

- Keep bear spray inside your tent, next to your hand.
- Sleep with a headlamp around your neck or within reach.
- If a bear is investigating your tent, speak in a firm voice. Bang pots.
- If the bear begins tearing into the tent, use bear spray immediately and exit the opposite side.
- Any bear that enters your tent at night is exhibiting predatory behavior. Fight back.
- Run your headlamp. Bears generally avoid confrontation when they lose the advantage of darkness.

## Camp Setup in Bear Country

Follow the **bear-muda triangle** layout:

1. **Cooking area:** Downwind from sleeping area. At least 200 ft (60 m) away.
2. **Food storage:** At least 200 ft (60 m) from both cooking and sleeping areas. Use a bear hang, canister, or locker.
3. **Sleeping area:** Upwind. Away from trails, water sources, and berry patches.

Do NOT camp on game trails, near salmon streams, or in areas with abundant fresh bear sign. Choose open sites where you have good visibility.

## Common Mistakes

- **Relying on color to identify the species.** Color is unreliable. Use the shoulder hump, face profile, and ear shape.
- **Playing dead with a black bear.** This is almost always fatal. Fight back.
- **Running from any bear.** Bears run at 35 mph (56 km/h). You cannot outrun them. Running triggers a pursuit response.
- **Climbing a tree.** Black bears are excellent climbers. Grizzlies can climb or shake you out. This is not a reliable escape.
- **Storing food in your tent or vehicle with open windows.** Bears that obtain human food become habituated and are eventually destroyed.
- **Keeping bear spray in your pack.** You have 2–5 seconds to respond. An inaccessible canister is a useless canister.
- **Approaching a bear for photographs.** Maintain at least 100 yd (91 m) distance.
