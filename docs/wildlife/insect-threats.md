# Insect and Arthropod Threats

> Insects and arachnids kill more people than every other animal on Earth combined — prevention is everything.

## At a Glance

- Mosquitoes are the deadliest animal on the planet: over 700,000 human deaths annually via disease transmission.
- Ticks transmit Lyme disease to approximately 476,000 people per year in the US alone.
- Anaphylaxis from bee and wasp stings kills more Americans annually than snakes, spiders, bears, and mountain lions combined.
- DEET remains the gold standard for insect repellent. Apply it to skin. Apply permethrin to clothing.
- Proper tick removal (fine-tipped tweezers, steady upward pull) prevents most disease transmission.

## Mosquitoes

### Disease Vectors

Mosquitoes transmit some of the most lethal pathogens on the planet:

| Disease | Regions | Symptoms | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Malaria | Sub-Saharan Africa, South/SE Asia, Central/South America | Cyclic fever, chills, anemia, organ failure | ~600,000 deaths/year. Prophylaxis available. |
| Dengue | Tropics worldwide, expanding range | High fever, severe joint pain ("breakbone fever"), hemorrhagic in severe cases | No specific treatment. Second infection more dangerous. |
| Zika | Americas, SE Asia, Pacific Islands | Mild fever, rash, joint pain | Critical risk for pregnant travelers (microcephaly). |
| West Nile | US, Europe, Africa, Middle East | Usually asymptomatic. Severe: encephalitis, meningitis | Most common mosquito-borne illness in continental US. |
| Yellow Fever | Sub-Saharan Africa, South America | Fever, jaundice, hemorrhage, organ failure | Vaccine available and required for some countries. |
| Chikungunya | Africa, Asia, Americas | Fever, severe joint pain lasting weeks to months | No vaccine, no specific treatment. |

### Prevention

#### Chemical Repellents

- **DEET (N,N-Diethyl-meta-toluamide):** Apply to exposed skin. Concentrations of 20–30% provide 6–8 hours of protection. Higher concentrations last longer but are not more effective per hour. Safe for adults and children over 2 months.
- **Picaridin:** Apply to skin. 20% concentration matches DEET effectiveness. Odorless, does not damage plastics or synthetics. Preferred by many travelers.
- **Oil of Lemon Eucalyptus (OLE/PMD):** Plant-derived. 30% concentration provides 4–6 hours of protection. Not as long-lasting as DEET. Do NOT use on children under 3 years.
- **IR3535:** Available in some commercial formulations. Moderate effectiveness, shorter duration.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    "Natural" repellents (citronella, lavender, peppermint, etc.) provide minimal protection lasting 20–60 minutes at most. Do NOT rely on them in areas with disease-carrying mosquitoes.

#### Permethrin-Treated Clothing

- Apply 0.5% permethrin spray to clothing, tents, mosquito nets, and gear. Allow to dry fully before wearing.
- Treated fabric kills mosquitoes, ticks, and other arthropods on contact.
- Lasts through 5–6 wash cycles (spray-applied) or 70+ washes (factory-treated, e.g., Insect Shield).
- Do NOT apply permethrin to skin. It is a contact insecticide, not a repellent for skin use.
- Combine with DEET on exposed skin for maximum protection.

#### Physical Barriers

- Sleep under a mosquito net. Treat the net with permethrin. Ensure edges are tucked under the mattress or sleeping pad.
- Wear long sleeves and long pants during peak mosquito hours (dusk through dawn for most species).
- Use head nets in areas of extreme mosquito density.
- Screen tent doors and windows. Repair any holes in netting.

#### Environmental Controls

- Eliminate standing water around camp: empty containers, overturn buckets, drain puddles.
- Camp on high ground with breeze when possible. Mosquitoes are weak fliers.
- Build a smoky fire (add green wood or damp leaves) upwind of your sitting area. Smoke repels mosquitoes.
- Avoid camping near stagnant water, swamps, or marshes.

## Ticks

### Disease Threats

| Disease | Tick Species | Region | Key Symptom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lyme disease | Blacklegged (deer) tick | Northeast, upper Midwest US; Europe | Bull's-eye rash (erythema migrans) in ~70% of cases |
| Rocky Mountain spotted fever | American dog tick, Rocky Mountain wood tick | Eastern US, despite the name | Spotted rash, high fever, can be fatal without treatment |
| Anaplasmosis | Blacklegged tick | Northeast, upper Midwest US | Fever, headache, muscle pain |
| Ehrlichiosis | Lone star tick | Southeast, south-central US | Fever, headache, fatigue |
| Babesiosis | Blacklegged tick | Northeast, upper Midwest US | Malaria-like: fever, chills, anemia |
| Alpha-gal syndrome | Lone star tick | Eastern US | Delayed allergic reaction to red meat, 3–6 hours after eating |

### Tick Removal

Use fine-tipped (pointed) tweezers. Blunt tweezers crush the tick body and may force pathogens into the wound.

1. Grasp the tick as close to the skin surface as possible. Grip the mouthparts, not the body.
2. Pull upward with steady, even pressure. Do NOT twist, jerk, or rock the tick.
3. If the mouthparts break off in the skin, remove them with tweezers if easily accessible. If not, leave them alone — the skin will expel them naturally.
4. Clean the bite area with rubbing alcohol, iodine, or soap and water.
5. Dispose of the tick: submerge in alcohol, flush down a toilet, or seal in tape. Do NOT crush between your fingers.
6. Save the tick in a sealed bag with a damp cotton ball if you want it identified or tested later. Note the date.

**What NOT to do:**
- Do NOT apply petroleum jelly, nail polish, heat (match, cigarette), or other "suffocation" methods. These delay removal and may cause the tick to regurgitate pathogens into the wound.
- Do NOT squeeze the tick's body.
- Do NOT use bare fingers to remove a tick if possible (use tweezers, or a cloth/leaf as a barrier).

### Tick Check Protocol

Perform a full-body tick check within 2 hours of leaving tick habitat. Ticks generally take 24–36 hours of attachment to transmit Lyme disease. Earlier removal dramatically reduces transmission risk.

Check these areas carefully:

- Scalp and hairline (run fingers through hair).
- Behind and inside the ears.
- Neck, especially the nape.
- Armpits.
- Inside the navel.
- Waistband and belt line.
- Groin and inner thighs.
- Behind the knees.
- Between the toes.

Shower within 2 hours of returning indoors. Tumble-dry clothing on high heat for 10 minutes to kill ticks (washing alone does not reliably kill them — it is the heat that matters).

### Tick Prevention

- Wear permethrin-treated clothing. This is the single most effective tick prevention measure.
- Apply DEET or picaridin to exposed skin.
- Tuck pants into socks. Tuck shirt into pants. Wear light-colored clothing to spot ticks.
- Walk in the center of trails. Avoid brushing against vegetation.
- Avoid sitting directly on logs, stone walls, or the ground in tick habitat.

## Bees, Wasps, and Hornets

### Anaphylaxis Recognition

Anaphylaxis is a severe, whole-body allergic reaction that can kill within minutes. Recognize these signs:

- **Skin:** Hives, flushing, swelling (especially face, lips, tongue, throat).
- **Respiratory:** Wheezing, shortness of breath, throat tightness, stridor (high-pitched breathing), coughing.
- **Cardiovascular:** Rapid pulse, low blood pressure, dizziness, fainting.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramping, diarrhea.
- **Neurological:** Confusion, anxiety, sense of impending doom.

Two or more body systems affected after a sting = anaphylaxis. Treat immediately.

### EpiPen Use

1. Remove the blue safety cap.
2. Grip the orange tip end firmly in your fist. Do NOT put your thumb over the end.
3. Swing and press firmly into the outer thigh (through clothing if necessary).
4. Hold in place for 10 seconds.
5. Remove and massage the injection site for 10 seconds.
6. Note the time. **If symptoms have not clearly improved after 5–15 minutes, give a second dose.**
7. **Always evacuate to a medical facility after using an EpiPen**, even if symptoms resolve completely.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    A **biphasic reaction** — anaphylaxis returning after it appeared to resolve — typically strikes **1–12 hours later**, and can occur up to 72 hours after the first reaction. Do not confuse this with the 5–15 minute window for a second dose.

    Someone who feels fine 20 minutes after epinephrine is **not** in the clear. They need to be somewhere with medical support and a second dose available for at least the next several hours.

### Field Treatment Without EpiPen

If no EpiPen is available:

- Administer 50 mg diphenhydramine (Benadryl) orally if the victim can swallow.
- Position the victim on their back with legs elevated (shock position). If vomiting, turn on their side.
- If the victim carries an asthma inhaler, administer 2 puffs. Albuterol helps with bronchospasm.
- Keep the victim warm.
- Prepare for CPR if breathing stops.
- Evacuate immediately. This is a life-threatening emergency.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Antihistamines (Benadryl) do NOT stop anaphylaxis. They only manage mild allergic reactions. Without epinephrine, severe anaphylaxis is likely fatal. Carry an EpiPen if you have any history of severe sting reactions.

### Sting Prevention

- Avoid bright-colored clothing and floral patterns.
- Do not wear perfume, cologne, or scented products.
- Cover food and drinks outdoors. Bees and wasps are attracted to sugar.
- Remain calm around individual bees/wasps. Swatting provokes them.
- If a swarm attacks, run in a straight line. Cover your face. Do NOT jump into water — the swarm will wait. Seek an enclosed space (vehicle, building, tent).
- Yellowjackets nest in the ground. Watch for ground holes and heavy insect traffic near the trail.

### Stinger Removal

Honeybees leave barbed stingers embedded in the skin. Remove the stinger as quickly as possible by any method — scrape, flick, or pull. Speed matters more than technique. Each second the stinger remains, more venom is injected from the attached venom sac.

## Scorpions

### Dangerous Species

Most scorpion stings are painful but not dangerous. The primary exception in North America:

- **Arizona bark scorpion** (*Centruroides sculpturatus*): Found in the southwestern US (Arizona, New Mexico, SE California, southern Nevada, Utah). 2–3 in (5–7.5 cm) long. Light brown to yellowish. Slender body with thin pincers.
- Worldwide, approximately 25 species of ~2,500 are dangerous. The *Androctonus* (North Africa/Middle East), *Tityus* (South America), and *Leiurus* (Middle East) genera contain the most lethal species.

### Habitat

- Under rocks, bark, and debris during the day.
- Inside boots, clothing, and bedding.
- Active at night. Glow under ultraviolet light — a UV flashlight is an effective detection tool.
- Excellent climbers. Can ascend walls, trees, and tent fabric.
- Attracted to moisture and shelter in arid environments.

### Sting Protocol

1. Clean the sting site with soap and water.
2. Apply ice wrapped in cloth, 10 minutes on, 10 minutes off.
3. Immobilize the affected limb.
4. Monitor for systemic symptoms: muscle twitching, roving eye movements (opsoclonus), difficulty swallowing, slurred speech, excessive salivation, respiratory distress.
5. Administer pain relief (ibuprofen, acetaminophen).
6. Seek medical care for bark scorpion stings, stings in children under 5, or any sting with systemic symptoms. Antivenom (Anascorp) is available for severe bark scorpion envenomation.
7. Most non-bark-scorpion stings resolve in 24–48 hours with pain management.

### Prevention

- Shake out boots, clothing, and sleeping bags every time.
- Sleep on a cot or elevated surface when possible in scorpion country.
- Check under and around your tent with a UV flashlight at night.
- Do not walk barefoot at night in scorpion habitat.
- Do not reach under rocks, logs, or debris without gloves.

## Fire Ants (Solenopsis invicta)

- **Range:** Southeastern US, expanding. Also South America, Australia, parts of Asia.
- **Behavior:** Extremely aggressive colony defense. Swarm and sting en masse when the mound is disturbed.
- **Sting:** Each ant bites to anchor, then stings repeatedly in a circular pattern. Venom is alkaloid-based.
- **Symptoms:** Immediate burning pain. Within 24 hours, characteristic white pustules form at each sting site. In sensitive individuals, anaphylaxis is possible — treat as per bee sting anaphylaxis protocol above.
- **Treatment:** Clean with soap and water. Apply ice. Do NOT pop the pustules (infection risk). Take antihistamines for itching. Monitor for anaphylaxis.
- **Prevention:** Watch for mounds (dome-shaped, 6–24 in / 15–61 cm tall, no visible entry hole on top). Avoid standing on or near mounds. If swarmed, brush ants off immediately — they grip with their jaws and will not release easily.

## Assassin Bugs and Chagas Disease

- **Range:** Americas, from the southern US to Argentina. Known as "kissing bugs" (*Triatoma* spp.) because they bite near the mouth at night.
- **Disease:** Chagas disease (*Trypanosoma cruzi*). Transmitted not through the bite itself but through the bug's feces, which are deposited near the bite wound. The victim scratches the bite, rubbing feces into the wound or mucous membranes.
- **Acute symptoms:** Swelling at the bite site (Romana sign if near the eye), fever, fatigue. Often asymptomatic.
- **Chronic disease:** 20–30% of infected individuals develop cardiac disease (cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias) or GI disease (megacolon, megaesophagus) years to decades later.
- **Prevention:** Sleep in tightly sealed shelters. Use mosquito netting tucked under the sleeping surface. Inspect bedding. Kissing bugs are nocturnal and hide in cracks in walls, thatched roofs, and behind furniture during the day.
- **If bitten:** Do NOT scratch. Clean the bite area thoroughly. Seek medical evaluation for testing if you are in an endemic area.

## Improvised Repellents

When commercial repellents are unavailable:

### Smoke

- Build a small, smoky fire (add green wood, damp leaves, or damp grass to a hot fire).
- Sit in the smoke plume. Smoke repels mosquitoes, black flies, and most biting insects.
- Dry your clothing over smoke to impart short-term repellent properties.
- Create a smudge pot: carry smoldering punk wood or a bundle of smoldering damp grass in a container.

### Mud

- Apply a thin layer of mud to exposed skin. The physical barrier deters biting insects.
- Reapply as it dries and cracks. Effectiveness is moderate and short-lived.
- Better than nothing in a no-repellent emergency.

### Plant-Based Options

These provide limited, short-duration (20–60 minute) protection:

- **Wild mint/pennyroyal:** Crush fresh leaves and rub on skin. Contains pulegone.
- **Yarrow:** Crush leaves and rub on exposed skin.
- **Birch bark:** Peel thin strips of papery bark and burn them for smoke. Contains compounds mosquitoes avoid.
- **Citronella grass (Cymbopogon nardus):** If available in tropical regions, crush and rub on skin.
- **Pine/spruce resin:** Apply sparingly to pulse points. Sticky but somewhat effective.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Plant-based repellents provide vastly inferior protection compared to DEET or picaridin. Do NOT rely on them in areas where mosquitoes carry malaria, dengue, or other life-threatening diseases. Use them only as a supplement or emergency backup.

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying DEET under clothing.** Apply to exposed skin only. Apply permethrin to clothing.
- **Using "suffocation" methods for tick removal.** Vaseline, heat, and nail polish delay removal and may increase disease transmission.
- **Ignoring mild sting reactions in someone with no allergy history.** Anaphylaxis can develop at any age with no prior warning. Always carry an EpiPen if traveling in remote areas.
- **Popping fire ant pustules.** Leads to secondary infection. Leave them intact.
- **Assuming mosquito nets alone are sufficient.** Nets must be permethrin-treated and properly tucked to be effective. Mosquitoes find gaps.
- **Scratching insect bites and assassin bug bites.** Scratching is the primary transmission route for Chagas disease and introduces bacteria into any bite wound.
- **Relying on citronella candles.** Studies show minimal effectiveness beyond a 3 ft (1 m) radius, and even that is marginal.

## Quick Reference

| Threat | Prevention | Immediate Treatment | Evacuate If |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mosquitoes | DEET on skin, permethrin on clothing, nets | N/A (treat diseases) | Fever develops after travel to endemic area |
| Ticks | Permethrin clothing, DEET, tick checks | Fine tweezers, steady pull, clean site | Bull's-eye rash, fever, or joint pain within 30 days |
| Bee/wasp stings | Avoid scents, cover food, stay calm | Remove stinger fast, ice, antihistamine | Any sign of anaphylaxis |
| Scorpions | Shake out boots, UV light checks, sleep elevated | Ice, immobilize, pain relief | Systemic symptoms, child under 5, bark scorpion |
| Fire ants | Avoid mounds, brush off immediately | Soap/water, ice, do not pop pustules | Anaphylaxis signs |
| Assassin bugs | Sealed shelter, netting, do not scratch | Clean bite, do not scratch, seek testing | Facial swelling (Romana sign), fever |
