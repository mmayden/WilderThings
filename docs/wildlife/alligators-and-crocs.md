# Alligator and Crocodile Safety

> Crocodilians have survived 200 million years unchanged — respect their territory, understand their behavior, and stay out of the water at the edges of their range.

## At a Glance

- Crocodiles are far more aggressive toward humans than alligators — know which species lives in your area
- Stay at least 20 feet (6 m) from alligators and 50 feet (15 m) from crocodiles on land
- Never swim, wade, or camp at the water's edge in crocodilian habitat, especially at dusk or night
- If grabbed, fight back aggressively — gouge the eyes and strike the snout
- Nesting females of both species are extremely aggressive and attack without provocation

## Species Identification

### Alligator vs. Crocodile

| Feature | Alligator | Crocodile |
|---|---|---|
| Snout shape | Wide, U-shaped | Narrow, V-shaped |
| Teeth visible (mouth closed) | Upper teeth only | Both upper and lower (interlocking) |
| Color | Dark gray to black | Olive, tan, or gray-green |
| Temperament | Generally shy of humans | Aggressive, especially saltwater crocs |
| Habitat | Freshwater (swamps, lakes, rivers) | Fresh, brackish, and saltwater |

!!! note
    If you can see the large fourth tooth on the lower jaw when the mouth is closed, it is a crocodile. Alligator jaws conceal the lower teeth.

### Key Species by Region

=== "North America"
    - **American alligator** — southeastern US, Florida to Texas. Up to 14 feet (4.3 m), 1,000 lbs (450 kg). Attacks occur but fatalities are rare (average 1 per year in the US).
    - **American crocodile** — southern Florida, Caribbean, Central America. Shy compared to saltwater and Nile crocodiles. Rarely attacks humans.

=== "Australia"
    - **Saltwater crocodile** — northern Australia, largest living reptile. Up to 23 feet (7 m), 2,200 lbs (1,000 kg). Extremely aggressive. Responsible for multiple fatalities per year across its range.
    - **Freshwater crocodile** — smaller, less aggressive, rarely dangerous to humans unless provoked.

=== "Africa"
    - **Nile crocodile** — sub-Saharan Africa. Up to 20 feet (6 m), 1,650 lbs (750 kg). Responsible for hundreds of human deaths annually. The most dangerous crocodilian to humans by fatality count.

=== "Southeast Asia"
    - **Saltwater crocodile** — same species as Australia. Found in India, Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia, and the Philippines. Equally dangerous.
    - **Mugger crocodile** — Indian subcontinent. Moderate aggression toward humans.

## Behavior

### Hunting Strategy

Crocodilians are ambush predators. They wait motionless at the water's edge, partially submerged, and strike explosively.

- Strike speed: a crocodilian can lunge its full body length in under one second.
- The "death roll" — spinning along the body's axis — is used to dismember prey. If grabbed, the animal will attempt to drag you into deep water.
- They hunt primarily by vibration and movement detection. Staying still at a safe distance reduces risk.

### When They Are Most Dangerous

- **Dusk, night, and dawn.** Peak hunting hours.
- **Nesting season.** Females guard nests aggressively. Nesting season varies by species and region (typically spring to early summer).
- **When habituated to humans.** Fed crocodilians lose fear of people and associate humans with food.
- **During drought or flooding.** Animals move into unusual areas, including roads, yards, and swimming pools.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Never feed alligators or crocodiles. A fed crocodilian is a dangerous crocodilian. It is also illegal in most jurisdictions.

## Encounter Protocol

### On Land

1. Back away slowly. Do not run unless the animal charges — then run in a straight line. The myth about running in zigzags is false; crocodilians are fast in a straight line but tire quickly over short distances.
2. Maintain at least 20 feet (6 m) from alligators and 50 feet (15 m) from crocodiles.
3. Do not block the path between the animal and the water. A crocodilian cut off from water is more dangerous.
4. If it hisses or lunges, you are too close. Move away immediately.

!!! tip
    Crocodilians on land are fast in short bursts — up to 11 mph (18 km/h) — but cannot sustain speed. A healthy adult human can outrun one in a straight line over 20–30 feet (6–9 m).

### In Water

- If you see a crocodilian in the water, exit immediately.
- Do not swim, kayak, or wade in known crocodilian habitat, especially in murky water.
- Keep hands and feet inside boats. Do not dangle limbs over the edge.
- Do not clean fish at the water's edge or discard fish scraps near camp.

### If Attacked

1. Fight back aggressively. Do not play dead — this is a predatory attack.
2. **Gouge the eyes.** This is the most effective defense. Use fingers, sticks, or any available tool.
3. **Strike the snout.** The snout is sensitive. Hit it with a fist, rock, or hard object.
4. **Pry the jaws.** If a limb is in the mouth, push down on the palatal valve (a flap at the back of the throat) — this causes the animal to release to avoid drowning.
5. If pulled into a death roll, do not resist the roll (you will lose the limb). Instead, roll with the animal and continue attacking the eyes.
6. Once free, get to land immediately and apply direct pressure to wounds. Seek emergency medical care — crocodilian bites cause severe infection.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Saltwater and Nile crocodile attacks are frequently fatal. If you are in territory shared with these species, treat every body of water as dangerous until confirmed otherwise.

## Water Safety in Reptile Territory

1. Never swim in freshwater in known crocodilian range. This includes lakes, rivers, billabongs, estuaries, and coastal areas (saltwater crocs hunt in the sea).
2. Do not collect water, wash dishes, or bathe at the same spot repeatedly. Crocodilians learn patterns.
3. Stand back from the water's edge — at least 6 feet (2 m) from any bank.
4. Use a container on a rope to collect water rather than crouching at the waterline.
5. Keep children and pets away from the water's edge at all times.

## Nest Defense

- Female alligators and crocodiles guard nests fiercely. Nests are mounds of vegetation near water, typically 3–5 feet (1–1.5 m) across.
- If you see a nest mound, leave the area immediately. The mother is nearby and will charge.
- Baby crocodilians make chirping sounds. If you hear chirping near water, a nest is close. Back away.

!!! warning "CAUTION"
    Crocodilian mothers respond to distress calls from hatchlings. If you hear chirping or pick up a baby crocodilian, the mother will attack without hesitation.

## Camping Near Water

1. Camp at least 50 feet (15 m) from any water body in crocodilian habitat. In saltwater croc territory, 165 feet (50 m) is safer.
2. Do not store food or cook at the water's edge.
3. Avoid established game trails between land and water — crocodilians use these routes.
4. Use a flashlight at night when moving near water. Crocodilian eyes reflect red-orange in torchlight.
5. In northern Australia, treat every body of water — including the ocean — as a potential saltwater croc habitat.

## Common Mistakes

- **Swimming in freshwater in crocodilian range.** The single most common cause of fatal crocodilian attacks.
- **Crouching at the water's edge.** Collecting water, washing, or cleaning fish in this position mimics prey behavior.
- **Assuming alligators are harmless.** Alligators are less aggressive than crocodiles but still attack, especially when large, habituated, or near a nest.
- **Running in zigzags.** This myth wastes energy. Run in a straight line away from the animal.
- **Returning to the same water access point repeatedly.** Crocodilians memorize patterns. Vary your approach.
- **Camping too close to water.** A 10-foot (3 m) buffer is not enough. Crocodiles can cover that distance before you react.

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Crocodilian on land nearby | Back away slowly, do not block path to water |
| Crocodilian charges on land | Run in a straight line away |
