# Natural Disaster Survival

> Immediate response actions for earthquakes, tornadoes, hurricanes, floods, wildfires, tsunamis, landslides, and volcanic eruptions — what to do in the first minutes.

## At a Glance
- Every disaster has a critical window of seconds to minutes where the right action saves your life.
- Earthquakes: Drop, Cover, Hold On. Do not run outside.
- Floods kill more people than any other natural disaster in the U.S. Never drive or walk through floodwater.
- Tsunamis: Move to high ground immediately upon warning or after prolonged shaking near a coast.
- Evacuation when ordered beats shelter-in-place for every disaster except tornadoes actively overhead.

## Earthquakes

### During the Shaking

1. **Drop** to your hands and knees. This prevents being knocked down.
2. **Cover** your head and neck under a sturdy table or desk. If no furniture is available, crouch against an interior wall and cover your head with your arms.
3. **Hold On** to your shelter and be prepared to move with it.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Do not run outside during shaking. Falling debris, glass, and building facades kill more people outside than collapsing structures kill inside.

### Myths to Ignore
- **Doorways are not safer** than other parts of a modern building. Get under a table.
- **The "triangle of life"** theory (lying beside large objects) is not supported by earthquake engineering research. Drop, Cover, Hold On remains the evidence-based response.

### After the Shaking

1. Expect aftershocks. Drop, Cover, Hold On again when they hit.
2. Check for injuries. Apply first aid (see [First Aid Basics](../medical/first-aid-basics.md)).
3. Check for gas leaks (smell), electrical damage (sparks), and structural cracks. Evacuate if any are present.
4. Do not use elevators.
5. Move to open ground away from damaged buildings.

## Tornadoes

### Shelter Selection

=== "In a Building"
    - Go to the lowest floor. Basements are ideal.
    - Move to an interior room with no windows: bathroom, closet, hallway.
    - Get under sturdy furniture or crouch low and cover your head.
    - Cover yourself with a mattress, heavy blankets, or pillows to protect from debris.

=== "In a Vehicle"
    - Do not try to outrun a tornado unless you can see open road and the tornado is far away and moving perpendicular to your path.
    - If the tornado is close, park the car, keep your seatbelt on, duck below the windows, and cover your head.
    - Alternatively, abandon the vehicle and lie flat in a low ditch or depression. Cover your head with your arms.

=== "Outdoors"
    - Lie flat in the lowest available ground — a ditch, ravine, or culvert.
    - Cover your head and neck with your arms.
    - Do not shelter under a highway overpass. Wind accelerates through the constriction.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Highway overpasses are death traps in tornadoes. Wind speeds increase through the narrow passage and debris channels directly through them.

## Hurricanes

### Preparation Timeline

| Timeframe | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| 72 hours out | Fill prescriptions, fuel vehicles, withdraw cash |
| 48 hours out | Board windows, bring outdoor items inside, charge all devices |
| 24 hours out | Fill bathtubs with water (flushing toilets), freeze water bottles (cooler ice) |
| Evacuation ordered | Leave immediately on designated routes. Do not delay. |

### During the Storm

- Stay away from windows.
- Move to an interior room on the lowest floor.
- If flooding begins, move to the highest floor. Do not go into the attic unless you have a tool to break through the roof.
- The "eye" brings a calm period. Do not go outside — the back half of the storm arrives suddenly with wind from the opposite direction.

### After the Storm

- Assume all standing water is contaminated (sewage, chemicals, debris).
- Do not touch downed power lines or anything they contact.
- Boil all drinking water until municipal supply is confirmed safe.

## Floods

### Flash Floods

Flash floods arrive in minutes with little warning. They are the deadliest flood type.

1. Move to high ground immediately. Do not wait to gather belongings.
2. Do not walk through moving water. Six inches (15 cm) of fast-moving water knocks an adult off their feet.
3. Do not drive through flooded roads. Twelve inches (30 cm) of moving water carries a car. Two feet (60 cm) floats a truck or SUV.
4. If trapped in a vehicle in rising water, unbuckle, open the window (not the door — water pressure holds doors shut), and climb onto the roof. Swim to safety only if you must.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    "Turn around, don't drown." More than half of U.S. flood deaths occur in vehicles. If water covers the road, choose a different route. Every time.

### Slow-Rise Floods

1. Move critical supplies to the highest floor.
2. Shut off electricity at the breaker if water enters your home.
3. Evacuate before water becomes too deep to walk through.
4. Do not return home until authorities confirm safety.

## Wildfires

### Evacuation

Evacuate early. Do not wait to see flames.

1. When a wildfire is reported in your area, load vehicles and prepare to leave.
2. Wear long sleeves, long pants, boots, and a cotton or wool face covering (not synthetic — it melts).
3. Close all windows and doors in your home but leave them unlocked for firefighters.
4. Drive with headlights on and windows up through smoke. Follow designated evacuation routes.

### Shelter-in-Place (Last Resort Only)

If trapped and unable to evacuate:

1. Move to a cleared area — a parking lot, pond, river, or plowed field.
2. If in a building, close all windows and doors. Fill sinks and tubs with water. Move furniture away from windows.
3. If caught in a vehicle, park in a cleared area away from vegetation. Close all vents and windows. Lie below the window line. The car will get extremely hot but may survive a fire front passing over.
4. If on foot in the open, find a depression or ditch. Lie face-down, cover exposed skin, and breathe through a wet cloth close to the ground where air is clearest.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Sheltering in place during a wildfire is a last resort when all evacuation routes are blocked. Survival is not guaranteed. Evacuate early and avoid reaching this point.

## Tsunamis

### Warning Signs

- A strong earthquake near the coast lasting 20+ seconds.
- Sudden, unusual recession of ocean water (the sea pulls back far beyond normal tide).
- A loud roaring sound from the ocean.
- Official warning sirens or alerts.

### Response

1. Move inland or to high ground — at least 100 feet (30 m) elevation or 2 miles (3 km) inland.
2. Move on foot if roads are gridlocked. Do not wait in traffic.
3. If unable to reach high ground, go to the highest floor of a reinforced concrete building. Stay away from windows.
4. Do not return to low ground after the first wave. Tsunamis arrive as a series of waves over hours. Later waves are often larger.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    If the ocean recedes suddenly and dramatically, you have minutes — possibly seconds — before the wave arrives. Run to high ground immediately. Do not stop for anything.

## Landslides

### Warning Signs

- Prolonged heavy rain on steep slopes.
- New cracks in the ground, pavement, or building foundations.
- Tilting trees, fences, or utility poles.
- Unusual sounds: rumbling, cracking, or trees snapping uphill.

### Response

1. Move laterally — perpendicular to the path of the slide, not downhill.
2. If escape is impossible, curl into a tight ball and protect your head with your arms.
3. After the slide, stay clear of the slide area. Secondary slides are common.
4. Check for injured people without entering unstable debris.

## Volcanic Eruptions

### Ash Fall

1. Stay indoors. Close all windows and doors. Seal gaps with damp towels.
2. Wear an N95 mask or wet cloth over your nose and mouth if outside. Volcanic ash is abrasive glass — it damages lungs.
3. Protect your eyes with goggles or glasses.
4. Do not drive unless necessary. Ash clogs engines and reduces visibility to near zero.
5. Clear ash from roofs before it accumulates — 4 inches (10 cm) of wet ash can collapse a roof.

### Lava Flows and Pyroclastic Flows

- Lava flows are slow enough to outrun on foot. Move uphill and perpendicular to the flow.
- Pyroclastic flows (superheated gas and rock, 300+ mph / 480+ km/h) are unsurvivable in the open. Your only option is to not be in their path. Evacuate early.

### Lahars (Volcanic Mudflows)

- Lahars follow river valleys at high speed. Move to high ground above the valley floor immediately.
- Do not cross bridges over rivers draining a volcano — lahars destroy bridges and anyone on them.

## Common Mistakes
- Running outside during an earthquake (falling debris kills).
- Driving through floodwater (the number one flood killer).
- Sheltering under a highway overpass during a tornado (wind acceleration and debris funneling).
- Returning to low ground after a single tsunami wave (later waves are often larger).
- Waiting to see wildfire flames before evacuating (smoke and ember showers arrive first and block escape routes).
- Ignoring landslide warning signs (cracks, tilting trees, unusual sounds).
- Going outside during the eye of a hurricane (the back half of the storm arrives suddenly).
- Treating volcanic ash as harmless dust (it is abrasive glass that damages lungs, eyes, and engines).

|----------|-----------------|--------|
| Earthquake | Drop, Cover, Hold On | Run outside |
| Tornado | Lowest floor, interior room, cover head | Shelter under overpass |
