# Lost in the Woods
> A step-by-step survival walkthrough from the moment you realize you are lost through rescue or self-extraction.

## At a Glance
- Apply the STOP protocol immediately: Sit, Think, Observe, Plan.
- Shelter and warmth kill faster than hunger — prioritize them over food.
- Stay put unless you have a compelling, logical reason to move.
- Make yourself visible and audible to search and rescue teams.
- Manage your psychology as aggressively as you manage your resources.

---

## The STOP Protocol

### S — Sit Down
Stop moving the instant you realize you are lost. Every step you take from this point without a plan increases the search area SAR must cover. Sit on your pack, a log, or the ground. Drink water if you have it.

### T — Think
Recall your last known position. Think about landmarks you passed. Estimate how long you have been walking since your last confirmed location. Do not let panic override this step.

### O — Observe
Survey your immediate surroundings. Note terrain features: ridgelines, water, trails, clearings. Check the sun's position to establish rough cardinal directions. Listen for roads, rivers, trains, or human activity. Inventory everything on your person and in your pockets.

### P — Plan
Decide: stay or move. Base this decision on the decision tree below, not on emotion.

---

## Stay Put vs. Move: Decision Tree

Answer these questions in order:

1. **Does anyone know your planned route and expected return time?**
   - Yes: Stay put. SAR will search your planned route first.
   - No: Continue to question 2.

2. **Do you have a confirmed direction to a road, trail, or town within 3 miles (5 km)?**
   - Yes: Consider moving, but leave markers at your current position.
   - No: Stay put.

3. **Is your current location dangerous (flash flood zone, exposed ridge in lightning, unstable slope)?**
   - Yes: Move to the nearest safe location, then stay put.
   - No: Stay put.

4. **Have you been waiting more than 72 hours with no sign of rescue and no one knows you are missing?**
   - Yes: Consider self-extraction by following water downhill.
   - No: Stay put.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    The urge to "just walk out" kills more lost people than exposure. Statistically, staying put is almost always the correct decision if anyone knows your approximate area.

## Immediate Actions (First 2 Hours)

### 1. Assess Resources on Your Person
Empty every pocket. Check your pack thoroughly. Common overlooked items:
- **Cell phone — try to call emergency services even with no bars.** Two separate things
  can save you here: an emergency call routes through *any* carrier's tower, not just
  yours, and on a recent iPhone, Pixel, or Galaxy the phone falls back to **satellite**
  automatically when no tower answers at all. Most people carrying that capability do not
  know they have it. See
  [Satellite SOS Built Into Your Phone](../navigation/gps-and-electronics.md#satellite-sos-built-into-your-phone).
- Lighter, matches, or ferrocerium rod
- Knife or multitool (see [Knife Use and Care](../tools-and-craft/knife-use-and-care.md))
- Cordage or paracord (see [Cordage](../tools-and-craft/cordage.md))
- Trash bag or emergency blanket
- Whistle
- Mirror or reflective surface
- Snack food or water

### 2. Establish Your Shelter Site
Select a location that is:
- Protected from wind (leeward side of a hill, dense tree stand)
- Near water but above flood level — at least 20 ft (6 m) above the waterline
- Visible from the air if possible (near a clearing)
- Away from dead standing trees (widowmakers)

### 3. Build or Improvise Shelter
Cold, wet ground pulls heat out of you far faster than cold air does — the 25x figure often quoted belongs to full water immersion, but wet ground is the same mechanism at lower intensity. Insulate from below first.
- **Leaf litter bed:** Pile dry leaves, pine needles, or grass at least 6 in (15 cm) deep.
- **Debris hut:** Ridgepole at 45 degrees, ribs of branches on both sides, piled with leaves and brush. Body-width only — smaller is warmer.
- **Trash bag shelter:** Cut open a bag for a lean-to or wrap yourself in it as a vapor barrier.

### 4. Start a Fire
Fire provides warmth, water purification, signaling, and morale. Build it near your shelter but not so close that sparks threaten your debris hut.
- Gather tinder, kindling, and fuel before striking your first spark.
- Three fires in a triangle (spaced about 100 ft / 30 m apart) is a recognized distress signal. Spacing matters — set too close together they read as one fire from the air.
- Add green branches or damp leaves to a hot fire to produce signal smoke.

## Water Priority

You can survive roughly 3 days without water, but your cognitive function degrades within hours of dehydration. Find water within the first day.

### Finding Water
- Follow animal trails downhill — they converge on water.
- Listen for running water; sound carries far in quiet woods.
- Look for green vegetation concentrated in a line (indicates subsurface water).
- Collect morning dew by dragging a cloth through grass, then wring it into your mouth.
- Rain catchment using a tarp, poncho, trash bag, or large leaves funneled into a container.

### Purifying Water
- **Boiling:** Roll boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft / 2,000 m). This is the most reliable field method.
- **No container for boiling?** Use a rock with a natural depression, heat stones in a fire, and drop them into the water (hot rock boiling).
- **Chemical treatment:** 2 drops of plain unscented household bleach per quart/liter
  (8 drops per gallon at 6%, 6 drops at 8.25%). Double the dose and the wait if the water
  is cloudy or very cold. Wait 30 minutes; the water should smell faintly of chlorine.
  Scented, "splashless", or colour-safe bleach is toxic — see
  [Water Purification](../water/purification.md#household-bleach-sodium-hypochlorite).

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Do not drink untreated water from streams or ponds unless you are in immediate danger of dying from dehydration. Giardia, cryptosporidium, and bacterial infections will make your situation dramatically worse within 24-48 hours.

    **Bleach does not kill cryptosporidium**, and neither does iodine. If crypto is your
    concern, boil or filter — chemical treatment alone is not enough. Boiling handles
    everything on this list, which is why it is the method to reach for first.

## Signaling

### Ground-to-Air Signals
- Clear a large area and arrange rocks, logs, or trampled snow into a V (need assistance) or X (need medical help). Make each arm at least 10 ft (3 m) long.
- Use contrasting colors: dark materials on snow, light materials on dark ground.

### Audible Signals
- Three blasts on a whistle = universal distress. Repeat every few minutes.
- Yell, bang pots, or strike rocks together if you have no whistle.
- Sound carries best at dawn and dusk.

### Visual Signals
- Signal mirror: aim reflected sunlight at aircraft by holding the mirror near your face, extending your other hand to frame the aircraft between your fingers, and sweeping the reflected light across your fingers. Effective up to 10 miles (16 km).
- Smoky fire during the day. Bright fire at night.
- Hang brightly colored clothing in tree canopies near clearings.

### Electronic Signals
- Cell phone: airplane mode or off to preserve battery. Power up periodically at high points.
- If you have signal, send a text with GPS coordinates rather than calling — texts require less bandwidth and may get through when calls cannot.
- **Satellite SOS needs open sky.** If your phone supports it, a dense canopy will defeat
  it — which is the usual situation for someone lost in woods. Move to the most open
  ground you can safely reach (clearing, ridgeline, gravel bar, lake edge), mark your
  route, hold still, and follow the on-screen prompt. Give it minutes, not seconds.
- Send the message while you still have battery. Do not save it for a last resort.
- Keep the phone warm and against your body — cold destroys battery capacity.

## Navigation if You Decide to Move

Use these methods only if the decision tree above supports moving.

### Following Water Downhill
Water flows to civilization more often than not. Follow a stream or creek downstream. It will merge with larger waterways, and larger waterways attract roads, bridges, and towns.

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Do not follow water through deep gorges or box canyons. If terrain becomes impassable, climb above the waterway and parallel it from higher ground.

### Finding Trails
- Look for cut stumps, blazes on trees, or worn ground.
- Game trails may lead to water but not to civilization. Human trails are wider, more consistent in direction, and show boot prints or litter.

### Natural Navigation
- **Sun:** Rises approximately east, sets approximately west. In the Northern Hemisphere at midday, the sun is due south.
- **Stick shadow method:** Plant a stick upright. Mark the tip of its shadow. Wait 15 minutes. Mark the new shadow tip. A line from the first mark to the second runs approximately west-to-east (first mark = west).
- **Stars:** In the Northern Hemisphere, locate Polaris (North Star) by following the two "pointer stars" on the Big Dipper's cup edge. Polaris indicates true north.
- **Vegetation:** Moss grows on all sides of trees and is not a reliable indicator of direction. Disregard this myth.

### Marking Your Route
Leave clear markers as you travel so SAR can track you or so you can return if the route fails.
- Break branches at head height, bending them to point your direction of travel.
- Stack three rocks in a cairn at each turn.
- Drag a stick to leave a visible trail on soft ground.

## Day 1 vs Day 2+ Priorities

### Day 1
1. STOP protocol
2. Shelter and insulation from ground
3. Fire
4. Water procurement and purification
5. Signaling (visual and audible)

### Day 2+
1. Improve shelter (wind resistance, rain proofing)
2. Establish a reliable water source
3. Expand signaling (ground-to-air symbols, signal fire ready to light)
4. Forage only for easily identifiable, safe food (do not eat wild mushrooms or unfamiliar plants)
5. Maintain fire through the night
6. Consider snares or fishing if near water (see [Cordage](../tools-and-craft/cordage.md) for trap construction lines)

!!! danger "WARNING"
    Expending calories to hunt or forage aggressively before Day 3 is almost always counterproductive. Your body's fat reserves are substantial. Focus on water, warmth, and being found.

## Psychological Management

Fear and panic kill faster than cold or thirst. Manage your mental state deliberately.

- **Accept the situation.** Denial wastes time. Say out loud: "I am lost. I will survive this."
- **Set small tasks.** Gather firewood. Improve shelter. These give purpose and reduce helplessness.
- **Talk to yourself.** Narrate your actions. This maintains cognitive function and prevents spiraling thoughts.
- **Control breathing.** Box breathing: inhale 4 seconds, hold 4 seconds, exhale 4 seconds, hold 4 seconds. Repeat until your heart rate drops.
- **Sleep discipline.** Rest when dark. Sleep deprivation destroys judgment. Even in cold conditions, sleep in shifts (if alone, set small fires to keep waking you to tend them).
- **Assign meaning.** Think about who and what you are surviving for. People with strong reasons to live consistently outperform those without.
- **Reject the timeline.** Do not tell yourself "if I'm not rescued by tomorrow..." Set no deadline for hope.

## What Search and Rescue Actually Does

### How SAR Searches
- **Hasty teams** deploy first: small, fast groups checking the most likely locations (trails, campsites, water sources, your planned route).
- **Grid search** follows if hasty teams fail: crews walk in systematic patterns across the search area.
- **Air assets** (helicopters, drones, fixed-wing) scan open terrain, ridgelines, and waterways.
- **Dog teams** track your scent. If you stay put, your scent concentrates in one area. If you wander, you dilute it across a wide zone.

### SAR Timelines
- If you filed a trip plan, SAR may be notified within hours of your expected return.
- Without a trip plan, SAR depends on someone reporting you missing, which may take days.
- Most wilderness rescues occur within 72 hours of activation.
- SAR does not stop looking after a fixed period. Searches continue based on probability of detection and survivability estimates.

### How to Be Found
- Get into the open when you hear aircraft, and **move** — motion is what catches a
  searcher's eye far more than shape or colour does.
- Wave with both arms crossed overhead repeatedly (not one arm — one arm reads as a
  casual wave from a hiker who is fine).
- Only if you cannot stand or move: lie flat with arms and legs spread on contrasting
  ground, to present the largest possible outline. This is the fallback for an injured
  person, not the default.
- Keep your signal fire fueled and ready to ignite at the sound of aircraft or voices.
- Respond to shouts with three whistle blasts or three yells.
- At night, keep your fire burning. Fire is visible from the air for miles.

## Common Mistakes
- Walking aimlessly in hopes of stumbling onto a road.
- Abandoning a shelter to build a new one instead of improving the current one.
- Rationing water too aggressively — drink when thirsty, find more water.
- Eating snow directly (lowers core temperature; melt it first).
- Separating from your group if lost together.
- Discarding gear that seems useless (every item has potential survival applications).
- Giving up signaling after a few hours.
- Travelling at night over unfamiliar terrain.

## Quick Reference

| Priority | Timeframe       | Action                        |
|----------|-----------------|-------------------------------|
| 1        | Immediate       | STOP protocol                 |
| 2        | First 30 min    | Assess resources, pick shelter site |
| 3        | First 2 hours   | Build shelter, start fire     |
| 4        | First 6 hours   | Find and purify water         |
| 5        | Ongoing         | Signal (visual, audible, fire)|
| 6        | Day 2+          | Improve shelter, maintain water, forage |

**Rule of Threes:**
- 3 minutes without air
- 3 hours without shelter (in harsh conditions)
- 3 days without water
- 3 weeks without food
